# Academic Narrative CV: Professor Daniele De Santis

## Navigating Economic Valuation and Structural Dynamics in Regulated Systems

The research program of Professor Daniele De Santis is situated at the intersection of transport economics, infrastructure investment appraisal, public utility regulation, and applied operational research. His scholarship addresses a fundamental tension in public economics: how regulatory bodies, municipal authorities, and private operators make long-term, capital-intensive investment choices in the presence of severe technological, macroeconomic, and regulatory uncertainties. De Santis investigates how classical deterministic valuation tools often fail to capture the operational flexibility, structural heterogeneity, and risk profiles inherent in public networks undergoing ecological and digital transitions.

To resolve these challenges, De Santis develops and applies sophisticated quantitative frameworks, including Real Options Analysis, stochastic dynamic simulations, hybrid engineering-economic cost functions, and Stochastic Frontier Analysis. His work provides actionable, mathematically grounded decision-support tools that span urban transit decarbonization, standard cost benchmarking in rail transit, and the empirical assessment of price regulation in wholesale distribution markets, establishing a coherent body of work dedicated to optimizing public spending and service efficiency.

## Beyond Deterministic Appraisal: Methodological Foundations in Transit Investment

A foundational pillar of De Santis's scholarship involves the systematic diagnostic of evaluation methods used for local public transport investments. In 'Investments in local public transport: A systematic literature review on evaluation methods with a focus on the role of uncertainty' (2025), De Santis, Giagnorio, and Matteucci conduct a comprehensive synthesis of appraisal frameworks spanning Discounted Cash Flow techniques, Cost-Benefit Analysis, Total Cost of Ownership, Life Cycle Costing, and constrained optimization models. This work maps evaluation metrics across economic, environmental, technical, and infrastructural dimensions while charting the shift in appraisal objectives from narrow financial efficiency metrics to multidimensional goals encompassing decarbonization, social inclusion, and network resilience.

A central theoretical contribution of this line of inquiry is the critique of prevailing transit appraisal practices. The authors demonstrate that while local public transport systems operate in volatile environments characterized by rapid technological disruption and policy shifts, the majority of literature and industry practice continues to rely on static, deterministic methods. By documenting the limitations of conventional appraisal, De Santis establishes the formal necessity for dynamic approaches, such as scenario analysis, Monte Carlo simulation, and Real Option Analysis, thereby setting the methodological foundation for his empirical and computational modeling across public transport systems.

## Stochastic Valuation of Fleet Transitions: Real Options in Bus Decarbonization

Building directly upon the imperative for dynamic evaluation under uncertainty, De Santis advances an extensive research agenda on the economic optimization of bus fleet decarbonization. In 'Bus fleet decarbonization under macroeconomic and technological uncertainties: A real options approach to support decision-making' (2024), Avenali, De Santis, Giagnorio, and Matteucci develop a multi-option Least Squares Monte Carlo simulation framework to evaluate sequential fleet replacement decisions across diesel, hybrid electric, compressed natural gas, and battery electric buses. By incorporating stochastic paths for fuel prices, electricity tariffs, powertrain costs, and battery salvage values across inner-city, urban, suburban, and commuter routes, the model demonstrates potential cost savings of up to ten percent of annual Total Cost of Ownership, revealing how route typology and contract duration critically dictate the timing and economic viability of zero-emission fleet transitions.

De Santis extends this stochastic decision framework to the design of public policy and fiscal subsidies in 'Public funding for bus system decarbonization accounting for technological uncertainty and depot infrastructure upgrades' (2026), co-authored with Avenali, Giagnorio, and Matteucci. This study embeds depot infrastructure constraints and vehicle acquisition paths into an integrated Total Cost of Ownership and Real Options model applied to a representative urban transit system. By simulating operator adoption thresholds under technological uncertainty, the analysis evaluates high-subsidy regimes, such as existing United States funding structures covering up to eighty-five percent of costs, against alternative allocations. The findings identify an optimal configuration combining approximately sixty-five percent vehicle funding with depot-specific infrastructure grants spanning four to seventy-eight percent over a ten-year horizon, proving that calibrated, infrastructure-dependent funding mechanisms can achieve maximum decarbonization velocity at substantially reduced fiscal cost.

## Structural Heterogeneity and Cost Benchmarking in Urban Rail Transit

Parallel to his work on bus fleet electrification, De Santis addresses the operational and technological cost structures of urban rail systems. In 'A hybrid cost model for light and heavy metro services' (2025), Avenali, De Santis, D'Alfonso, Giagnorio, and Matteucci formulate a hybrid cost function that integrates economic accounting with technical parameters, such as automation levels, wheel technologies, and peak hourly capacities, utilizing comprehensive operating data from the universe of Italian metro networks. The study reveals fundamental structural divergences between light and heavy metro technologies, establishing that heavy metro services entail substantially higher capital intensity and train depreciation per vehicle-kilometer, yet yield superior cost efficiency on a per seat-kilometer basis when deployed in high-density corridors.

This modeling framework provides critical analytical benchmarks for transport authorities designing competitive tenders and setting public service compensations. Through sensitivity and scale analyses, the research demonstrates that standard unit costs are strongly responsive to train and driver productivity, scale economies, and station operating hours, with infrastructure maintenance efficiencies exerting a more pronounced cost-reduction effect than rolling stock maintenance. By formalizing these relationships, De Santis equips public regulators with robust tools to establish auction reserve prices and negotiate with local monopolistic transit operators.

## Regulatory Shocks, Production Frontiers, and Efficiency in Distribution Markets

Extending his analytical focus on regulated industries beyond passenger transport, De Santis investigates the microeconomic impacts of pricing reforms in wholesale supply chains. In 'The pharmaceutical distributors’ efficiency in Italy: an assessment of the impact of the 2010 reimbursable drug pricing reform' (2025), Matteucci and De Santis deploy Stochastic Frontier Analysis to examine an extensive panel dataset of Italian pharmaceutical wholesalers from 2009 to 2019. The study assesses the structural consequences of regulatory interventions that halved statutory distribution margins on reimbursable drugs.

The empirical findings demonstrate that drastic margin reductions failed to stimulate long-term market efficiency or enhance social welfare, as larger distribution firms systematically lagged behind smaller counterparts in technical efficiency. Moreover, the results indicate that even highly efficient wholesalers struggle to achieve normal accounting profits under margin structures that do not reflect operational distribution costs. This work provides an empirical critique of non-cost-oriented regulatory policies, highlighting the imperative for tariff structures that balance consumer protection with the operational viability of critical distribution intermediaries.
