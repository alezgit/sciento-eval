## Digital Architectures and Operational Resilience in Socio-Technical Systems

The academic trajectory of Professor Eugenio Oropallo is defined by an interdisciplinary investigation of digital transformation, knowledge governance, organizational resilience, and sustainable industrial operations. His scholarship bridges management engineering, computational informatics, and organizational behavior, examining how emerging technologies such as blockchain, Industry 4.0 paradigms, and advanced decision support systems can be systematically embedded within complex organizational and supply networks. By synthesizing rigorous empirical methods, structural modeling, system dynamics, and design science, Oropallo evaluates the socio-technical mechanisms that govern knowledge creation, cyber resilience, sustainable operations, and behavioral transitions in contemporary economies.

At the core of his work is a commitment to resolving operational and systemic friction points, ranging from information asymmetry in decentralized networks to risk mitigation in industrial process engineering. Oropallo consistently explores how technological infrastructures interact with human decision-making, organizational routines, and institutional frameworks, building analytical tools that translate technological capabilities into measurable strategic, environmental, and operational performance outcomes.

## Re-Engineering Knowledge Epistemologies and Distributed Digital Technologies

A foundational pillar of Oropallo’s scholarship addresses how digital transformation reshapes organizational knowledge governance and technological ecosystems. In 'The WISED knowledge-creating company: rethinking SECI model in light of the digital transition' (2024), he re-conceptualizes Nonaka’s classical epistemological and ontological knowledge creation model by introducing the WISED framework, which incorporates webification, informalisation, systematisation, explicitation, and digitalisation to account for modern technological realities. This theoretical broadening is complemented by meta-level epistemological inquiries, such as 'Knowing what you don’t know: a tertiary study on knowledge management' (2023), wherein he executes a tertiary review of over two decades of knowledge management literature to systematically categorize research frameworks, units of analysis, and thematic trajectories.

Oropallo extends these knowledge architectures into empirical organizational settings and decentralized networks. In 'Digital knowledge management in agile self-tuning organisations: a multiple case study' (2025), he utilizes a grounded theory approach to examine how Industry 4.0 systems enable agile knowledge-intensive firms to enhance operational coordination. In 'Customer knowledge management in SMEs facing digital transformation' (2020), he investigates the intersection of customer relationship management and knowledge systems across creative luxury enterprises, identifying adoption barriers stemming from vendor deficits. His exploration of decentralized ledgers is illustrated in 'Surfing blockchain wave, or drowning? Shaping the future of distributed ledgers and decentralized technologies' (2021), where bibliometric network analysis identifies core motor and specialized themes across the technological landscape. He applies these distributed concepts across diverse domains, including accounting fraud mitigation in 'Blockchain technology design in accounting: Game changer to tackle fraud or technological fairy tale?' (2022), circular supply chain governance via the Triple Retry framework in 'Blockchain technology for bridging trust, traceability and transparency in circular supply chain' (2022), the maritime sector in 'Sailing through a digital and resilient shipbuilding supply chain: An empirical investigation' (2023), the circular construction sector in 'What we learn is what we earn from sustainable and circular construction' (2023), and emerging virtual environments in 'A first bibliometric literature review on Metaverse' (2022) and 'Kick-start your scientific journey into the metaverse' (2023).

## Strategic Cybersecurity Governance, Organizational Learning, and Risk Resilience

Beyond technological design, Oropallo’s program investigates the internal capabilities and governance mechanisms required to safeguard digital systems against systemic vulnerabilities. In 'Is cybersecurity outsourcing always effective? Strategic organisational insights and knowledge in the era of digitalisation' (2026), he employs the NIST framework, Mann-Whitney U testing, and binary logistic regression to evaluate the strategic tradeoffs between internally managed cybersecurity and third-party outsourcing. His findings demonstrate that while external outsourcing enhances formal disciplinary protocols, internal management substantially improves tacit knowledge codification, log preservation, and experiential organizational learning, establishing cybersecurity as an active catalyst for enterprise resilience.

This concern for resilience is complemented by engineering methodologies aimed at mitigating operational hazards. In 'The application of System Dynamics to industrial plants in the perspective of Process Resilience Engineering' (2014), Oropallo applies system dynamics simulation and causal loop modeling to evaluate safety level erosion and dynamic stability loss in liquefied petroleum gas storage plants. Translating these risk perspectives into actionable operational tools, 'A decision support system to assess the operational safety and economic benefits of risk-based inspection implementation strategies' (2024) formulates a specialized decision support system grounded in API RP 581 standards. By modeling corrosion-based damage mechanisms on industrial heat exchangers, the system integrates non-destructive testing data to conduct cost-benefit analyses, directly optimizing maintenance schedules and minimizing costly plant shutdowns.
