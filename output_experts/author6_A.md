# Academic Narrative CV: Professor Chiara Grosso

## Architectures of Personalization: From Product Configuration to Sustainable Industrial Systems

Professor Chiara Grosso has established an interdisciplinary research agenda centered on the architectural, behavioral, and operational mechanisms of mass customization and digital product configuration. Her scholarship interrogates how digital interfaces mediate complex choice environments, translating consumer requirements into bespoke physical artifacts while balancing operational efficiency. Across her investigative trajectory, Professor Grosso has expanded the locus of product configurators from dyadic e-commerce sales tools into multi-actor decision-support systems that address closed-loop supply chains, circular product lifecycles, and sustainable industrial transformations. Methodologically, her work pairs rigorous psychometric and behavioral survey designs with structural equation modeling, multi-criteria decision analysis, and empirical software landscape audits to unpack how interface capabilities influence user cognition, organizational performance, and ecological stewardship.

At the conceptual core of her program is the tension between flexibility and complexity. Early investigations into customer value creation revealed that online configurators generate utility beyond functional fit, unlocking symbolic dimensions of uniqueness and self-expression. Over time, this focus on individual customer-interface dyads broadened into systemic inquiries into how configuration logic can be leveraged to govern interorganizational knowledge flows, advance the twin digital and green transition, and mitigate systemic waste in resource-intensive sectors. Her work constructs a continuous theoretical bridge connecting cognitive decision architectures, social interactivity, and industrial sustainability.

## The Operational and Perceptual Foundations of Sales Configuration

Professor Grosso's foundational research systematically deconstructed the relationship between web-based sales configurator capabilities and consumer-perceived product value. In 'Towards an understanding of how the capabilities deployed by a Web-based sales configurator can increase the benefits of possessing a mass-customized product' (2014), authored with Cipriano Forza and Alessio Trentin, she demonstrated through self-customization experiments that configurator capabilities differentially impact value dimensions. The authors established that flexible navigation, focused navigation, and easy comparison capabilities directly enhance uniqueness and self-expressiveness benefits alongside traditional utilitarian utility, whereas benefit-cost communication and user-friendly product space descriptions primarily augment utilitarian benefit. This empirical foundation was expanded in 'Enhancing the consumer-perceived benefits of a mass-customized product through its online sales configurator: An empirical examination' (2017), where Sandrin, Trentin, Grosso, and Forza evaluated configuration journeys across consumer product categories, confirming that toolkits that streamline navigation unlock elevated willingness-to-pay by elevating self-expressive satisfaction. Complementing these cross-sector evaluations, 'Shoe configurators: A comparative analysis of capabilities and benefits' (2017) benchmarked footwear toolkits against broader fashion configurators to diagnose industry-specific interface deficits.

Recognizing that front-end toolkits cannot operate in an operational vacuum, Professor Grosso extended her inquiry into organizational capabilities and manufacturing strategy. In 'Configuration challenges for the âMade in Italyâ Agrifood industry' (2021), co-authored with Fabrizio Checchinato, Vladi Finotto, and Christine Mauracher, she investigated small and medium-sized enterprises to evaluate the alignment between online sales configurators and consumer shopping habits, demonstrating how mass customization interfaces drive customer experience innovation in tradition-rich sectors. Moving to global manufacturing networks, Trentin, Sandrin, Suzic, Grosso, and Forza in 'Reconciling Product Flexibility with Cost, Delivery, and Quality: The Importance of Bundling Mass Customization Practices' (2025) leveraged covariance-based structural equation modeling across 213 manufacturing plants in 16 countries. Drawing upon resource orchestration theory and a fit-as-covariation perspective, the study demonstrated that bundling knowledge absorption from customers, product modularity, and configurator deployment accounts for 41.9 percent of the variation in mass customization capability, substantially exceeding the isolated effects of these practices and demonstrating the imperative of synchronized operational enablers.

## Social Software Integration and Collaborative Decision-Making

While traditional mass customization models assumed an isolated consumer interacting with an automated toolkit, Professor Grosso identified the profound limitations of this dyadic assumption. In 'Support for the social dimension of shopping through web based sales configurators' (2015) and 'Supporting the social dimension of shopping for personalized products through online sales configurators' (2017), co-authored with Forza and Trentin, she conducted an empirical analysis of 277 operational sales configurators, identifying eight distinct architectural modalities connecting configurators to social software applications. This taxonomy mapped how external social platforms fulfill user needs for social feedback and social involvement across differing stages of the configuration-shopping task, illustrating significant adoption variances across industrial sectors. Building directly upon this structural mapping, 'Users' social-interaction needs while shopping via online sales configurators' (2019) analyzed 943 configuration experiences, discovering that consumers experience heterogeneous social interaction needs across discrete stages of configuration and actively seek varied interaction channels depending on whether they are consulting peers, experts, or community members.

Professor Grosso formalized these insights into actionable interface paradigms in 'Enhancing users' digital social interactions while shopping via online sales configurators' (2023), wherein she proposed a web-based architectural feature that allows consumers to invite selected trustworthy shopping advisors directly into the ongoing personalization journey. This co-browsing configuration model reproduces the collaborative, reassuring dynamics of physical retail, mitigating consumer choice uncertainty and cognitive fatigue. Extending the psychological foundations of this work, 'Exploring Configurator Usersâ Motivational Drivers for Digital Social Interaction' (2021), written with Forza, analyzed 937 self-design episodes across 378 business-to-consumer configurators. The investigation illuminated the motivational mechanisms underlying users' psychological need for human social presence during technology-assisted value co-creation, demonstrating that social computing features provide essential emotional anchoring that elevates user satisfaction and engagement during complex customization tasks.

## Algorithmic Adaptation in Socio-Economic Systems

The configuration paradigms that Professor Grosso developed within industrial engineering found an innovative socioeconomic application in algorithmic labor support. In 'AI-implemented toolkit to assist users with career "configuration": The case of Create Your Own Future' (2022), written with Nahum Sazen and Roberto Boselli, she transposed the structural logic of modular configuration and rule-based decision support to labor market economics. Confronting the digital disruption of Industry 4.0, post-pandemic labor shifts, and refugee displacement, the study evaluated an artificial intelligence toolkit designed to help vulnerable, low-skilled workers configure personalized vocational roadmaps. By conceptualizing career trajectories through a customizable, modular framework, the work proved that digital configuration systems can foster individual career adaptability, reallocate human capabilities, and facilitate sustainable employment across shifting economic environments.

## The Twin Transition: Circular Configurators and Digital Product Passports

Over recent years, Professor Grosso has synthesized her configuration expertise with circular economy imperatives, spearheading research on how modular data systems govern the European Union twin green and digital transition. In 'Enabling Closed-Loop Collaboration Through Configurators: A Digital Strategy for Circular Platforms' (2026), co-authored with Ana Paula Henriques Trevisan, Marco Vitti, and Claudio Sassanelli, she investigated European circular platforms, re-engineering product configurators from tools of upfront personalization into mechanisms for component recovery, modular repair, and interorganizational industrial symbiosis. This logic was advanced in 'Designing for circularity: exploring configurator-based decision support for Eco-design in food packaging*' (2025), written with Marco Scatto and Katia Venturini. Aligning with the EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation, the authors mobilized the Analytic Hierarchy Process and Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis alongside expert evaluations to demonstrate how intelligent configurators embed lifecycle intelligence, comparing reuse and recycling pathways to guide circular packaging design at the earliest conceptual stage.

This circular framework extends across resource-intensive manufacturing sectors. In 'Advancing Business Strategy in End-Of-Life Management for the Fashion Industry' (2025), Bonifazi, D'Adamo, Grosso, and Palmieri utilized multi-criteria assessments to evaluate textile end-of-life alternatives, establishing reuse and mechanical recycling as dominant strategies over landfill disposal in alignment with Sustainable Development Goal 12. In 'An Integrated Business Strategy for the Twin Transition: Leveraging Digital Product Passports and Circular Economy Models' (2025), co-authored with Idiano D'Adamo, Luciano Fratocchi, and Mohammad Tavana, Professor Grosso applied Analytic Hierarchy Process and TOPSIS methodologies to show how Digital Product Passports (DPPs) serve as data governance instruments that operationalize traceability, repair guidance, and recycling protocols while mitigating information overload.

Her empirical scrutiny of Digital Product Passports deepened through consumer and organizational perspectives. In 'Digital product passports for cleaner production: Economic evidence from producers and consumers in the fashion industry' (2026), Becchi, Cascavilla, D'Adamo, and Grosso paired expert criteria with a 415-respondent consumer survey, demonstrating that digital transparency overcomes information asymmetries and generates a significant consumer willingness to pay a price premium. Concurrently, 'Integrating knowledge management and digital product passports to foster sustainable and collaborative ecosystems' (2026), co-authored with Becchi, D'Adamo, Di Leo, Gastaldi, and Trusiani, employed k-means clustering, principal component analysis, and logistic regression across fashion and cosmetics sectors to identify distinct consumer profiles and conceptualize the passport as a socio-technical knowledge infrastructure. Furthermore, 'Bridging digital tools and human skills: A sustainable competence framework for implementing digital product passports in the cosmetics industry' (2026), with Cascavilla, D'Adamo, and Trusiani, deployed multi-criteria methods (AHP, TOPSIS, VIKOR) to show that realizing the strategic value of digital passports requires cultivating human organizational competencies alongside digital infrastructure to anchor consumer brand trust.

Broadening this systemic circularity to extreme macro-environments, Professor Grosso contributed to resource recovery syntheses in infrastructure and aerospace. In 'Current trends and challenges in construction and demolition waste recycling' (2025), with Bonifazi, Palmieri, and Serranti, she evaluated artificial intelligence sorting tools and decision-support mechanisms to alleviate urban resource depletion. Taking closed-loop principles to extraterrestrial constraints, 'Circular space economy: A review of innovative sustainable waste management' (2026), authored with Bonifazi, D'Adamo, Palmieri, and Zorpas, executed a PRISMA-guided systematic review and VOSviewer bibliometric mapping of orbital debris mitigation. The study conceptualized space debris as in situ manufacturing feedstock, integrating hyperspectral sorting, biological carbon reactors, and additive manufacturing into a unified paradigm of orbital circularity.

## Toward Cyber-Physical Circular Intelligence

Professor Chiara Grosso's scholarship traces an intellectual progression from the micro-level psychology of consumer choice navigation to macro-level governance architectures for circular industrial ecosystems. By treating configurators not merely as marketing devices but as fundamental rule-based engines for managing complexity, her work reconciles the operational requirements of mass customization with planetary boundary constraints. Her future trajectory increasingly focuses on the confluence of artificial intelligence, modular data standards, and closed-loop lifecycle tracking, positioning digital product passports and collaborative configurators as foundational socio-technical infrastructures capable of guiding industry through the dual imperatives of ecological resilience and digital transformation.
