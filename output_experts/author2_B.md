# Academic Narrative CV: Professor Eugenio Oropallo

## Navigating Socio-Technical Systems: Knowledge Management, Digital Transition, and System Dynamics

Professor Eugenio Oropallo has established a comprehensive research program centered on the systemic evaluation of technology adoption, knowledge management, and operational risk across complex industrial, organizational, and digital environments. His work sits at the intersection of process engineering, information systems, supply chain orchestration, and organizational theory. By combining empirical quantitative models, design science research, and bibliometric syntheses, Oropallo investigates how emerging digital architectures—such as Industry 4.0 technologies, distributed ledger systems, and virtual platforms—reconfigure decision-making mechanisms, operational resilience, and value creation in diverse sectors ranging from manufacturing and logistics to healthcare and small business operations.

A unifying thread in Oropallo's scientific trajectory is the rigorous examination of how organizations capture, transfer, and safeguard knowledge during rapid technological transformations. Rather than treating technology adoption as a purely technical implementation, his research models the dynamic feedback loops, cognitive barriers, and governance structures that determine operational stability and strategic success. This systemic orientation links foundational inquiries into industrial plant safety and dynamic system stability with contemporary investigations into decentralized blockchain governance, healthcare data interoperability, and circular supply chain ecosystems.

## Systemic Risk and Decision Support: From Process Safety to Non-Intrusive Inspection

The foundation of Oropallo’s analytical framework is rooted in quantifying resilience erosion and dynamic risk within industrial process facilities. In 'The application of System Dynamics to industrial plants in the perspective of Process Resilience Engineering' (2014), the research demonstrates how classical risk assessment tools often fall short when dealing with unexpected initiating events and loss of dynamic system stability. By operationalizing System Dynamics and Causal Loop Diagrams within a Liquefied Petroleum Gas storage plant, this work establishes a method to quantify safety level erosion under fluctuating operational conditions, framing resilience as the systemic capacity to sustain operational safety under both anticipated and unanticipated disturbances.

This early commitment to quantifying operational trade-offs evolved into data-driven decision support systems designed to optimize maintenance scheduling and inspection economics. In 'A decision support system to assess the operational safety and economic benefits of risk-based inspection implementation strategies' (2024), Oropallo and his co-authors extend American Petroleum Institute API RP 581 standards by constructing a decision support platform that evaluates non-intrusive inspection methodologies. Applied to shell and tube heat exchangers facing corrosion-based damage mechanisms such as thinning and corrosion under insulation, the platform balances financial risk against operational safety, demonstrating how non-destructive testing reduces costly scheduled plant shutdowns without compromising integrity.

## Knowledge Management Architectures and Meta-Analytic Synthesis

Recognizing that operational resilience relies fundamentally on structural knowledge capabilities, Oropallo conducted extensive epistemological and bibliometric studies to map the trajectory of knowledge management research. In 'Knowing what you don’t know: a tertiary study on knowledge management' (2023), he synthesizes two decades of literature reviews through an integrated tertiary methodology, deploying bibliometric clustering alongside systematic analysis to identify sustained research streams alongside persistent theoretical blindspots. This structural diagnosis laid the groundwork for re-evaluating core organizational knowledge creation frameworks in light of modern digital transitions.

To accommodate the rapid digital transformation of enterprise ecosystems, Oropallo reinterprets classic knowledge creation models. This framework is extended into specialized sectors in 'Investigating Healthcare 4.0 Transition Through a Knowledge Management Perspective' (2023), where 4.0 enabling technologies are shown to restructure socialization, externalization, combination, and internalization in clinical settings. ## Organizational Transformation in SMEs: Dynamic Capabilities, Internationalization, and Customer Knowledge

Translating high-level knowledge creation frameworks into organizational practice, Oropallo focused on small and medium enterprises (SMEs) struggling with resource constraints, dynamic market expansion, and digital inertia. In 'Assessing SMEs' internationalisation strategies in action' (2020), a structured content analysis across major scientific databases maps the enablers, barriers, and phase transitions that SMEs encounter during cross-border growth, highlighting critical research gaps between early entrance and sustained international expansion.

Simultaneously, Oropallo examined how creative and traditional SMEs navigate digital adoption at the interface of customer management. In 'Customer knowledge management in SMEs facing digital transformation' (2020), an empirical survey of luxury handicraft and retail enterprises defines customer knowledge management as the structural intersection of customer relationship management and knowledge management. The study reveals a significant gap: despite the availability of cost-effective digital tools, SMEs exhibit tech inertia and rely heavily on traditional systems, pointing to decision-support deficits among IT vendors and underscoring the need for tailored dynamic capability development.

As centralized data management systems increasingly faced security, interoperability, and trust deficits, Oropallo shifted a major portion of his research toward decentralized architectures and blockchain technology. In 'Surfing blockchain wave, or drowning? Shaping the future of distributed ledgers and decentralized technologies' (2021), a comprehensive bibliometric performance and network analysis maps the research topology of distributed ledgers, identifying core research clusters and outlining how the key features of traceability, tracking, transparency, and trust can re-engineer transaction processes across organizational boundaries.

Building upon this foundational mapping, Oropallo applied Design Science Research (DSR) to build concrete distributed ledger solutions for administrative and medical governance. Expanding DSR into medical data management, 'Blockchain design in health data management' (2022) engineers a permissioned platform that resolves electronic health record fragmentation, permitting secure therapy tracking while eliminating transparency and privacy risks across the healthcare value chain.

In parallel, Oropallo extended decentralized ledger models to sustainable industrial loops. In 'Blockchain technology for bridging trust, traceability and transparency in circular supply chain' (2022), he formulates the integrated Triple Retry framework, which connects reverse logistics processes (recycling, redistribution, remanufacturing) with blockchain capabilities to achieve end-to-end material traceability and waste control across multi-actor supply chains.
