## Thematic Research Areas

### Multi-Dimensional DEA Performance Benchmarking
Non-parametric efficiency and productivity analysis applied with a consistent methodological signature: realism over abstraction, explicit conditioning on country- and DMU-level heterogeneity, and the abandonment of convexity assumptions where the underlying technology demands it. The conical FDH estimator of directional distances and Luenberger productivity indices, developed jointly with Daraio and Simar, is the technical foundation on which the country-level waste efficiency, municipal waste efficiency, and ATO-level eco-efficiency applications rest. The work extends DEA into the BSC/intellectual-capital and gender-dimension indicator space, where the relevant performance dimensions are intangible and the variables are proxy indicators. The methodological posture is to construct frontiers that are realistic and context-aware rather than smooth and abstract, and to surface efficiency targets that are actionable for the heterogeneous units under analysis.

## Academic Biography

# Simone Di Leo — An Academic Narrative

## The Core Question: Quantification Under Conditions of Heterogeneity

The work of Simone Di Leo is animated by a single, persistent intellectual tension: how can complex social, organizational, and environmental systems be measured, benchmarked, and compared when the units under analysis are intrinsically heterogeneous, when the relevant dimensions of performance resist straightforward observation, and when the very act of measurement is itself a contested act of construction? Across an unusually broad research portfolio — spanning efficiency analysis of Italian municipalities, the financial appraisal of photovoltaic and battery storage systems, the epistemology of university rankings, and the architectural design of historical data infrastructures — Di Leo has consistently returned to this question rather than retreating into methodological silos. The throughline is not a domain but a stance: quantitative rigor deployed self-consciously, with the awareness that every dataset is an artefact and every frontier is a constructed boundary.

This stance becomes visible first in the methodological commitments, then in the empirical applications, and finally in the meta-theoretical reflections that bookend the trajectory.

## From Data Plumbing to Knowledge Infrastructures

Di Leo's earliest work is grounded in the unglamorous but foundational problem of data quality. In "Accounting for quality in data integration systems: a completeness-aware integration approach" (2022), co-authored with Cinzia Daraio and Monica Scannapieco, the argument is that historical, multi-source data systems — those spanning space and time — cannot be treated as if they were single-source repositories. The proposed solution is architectural: the integration layer must propagate completeness information so that the final user, rather than confronting the burden of accessing each constituent database, encounters a layer that is *by design* as complete as possible and that discloses its own availability. Applied to a research infrastructure for science and innovation studies, the work advances the case for moving from data infrastructures towards *knowledge infrastructures* — a conceptual move that foreshadows much of what follows.

This is not a peripheral concern. It establishes a methodological disposition: that the construction of the data substrate is itself a substantive intellectual act, and that the user's interpretive capacity depends on what the system makes visible and what it occludes. The same disposition reappears later in "Flexible Techniques to Detect Typical Hidden Errors in Large Longitudinal Datasets" (2024), with Renato Bruni and Daraio, where the problem shifts from inter-source integration to intra-source temporal integrity. Here, the work is on *elusive* errors — those that survive generic quality screening because they mimic the natural signal of trends, fluctuations, and structural breaks. The proposed technique is generic in form (a system of indicators analyzing differences between consecutive values and their symmetries) but conceptually exacting: the goal is to distinguish error from genuine evolution rather than to flatten one into the other. Implemented on the ETER database of European Higher Education institutions, the approach operationalizes the principle that data cleaning is not the application of thresholds but a form of interpretation.

The data-infrastructure strand also surfaces in "An integrated dataset providing a landscape of the Italian Universities" (2023), with Bruni, Daraio, Ghamiloo, and Bianchi, where the deliverable is a multi-perspective dataset linking Scopus-mined research keyword profiles to USTAT administrative variables and unique university balance sheets. The contribution is the integration itself: scientific specialization, administrative structure, and economic position are made simultaneously available so that subsequent analyses need not rebuild the substrate from scratch.

## Benchmarking Knowledge-Based Organizations: The Meta-Theoretical Turn

Where the data-infrastructure work is technical in register, the work on university benchmarking is where Di Leo's epistemological commitments come most directly into view. "Meta-choices in ranking knowledge-based organizations" (2022), with Daraio, Iazzolino, Laise, and Coniglio, is not another ranking. It is a critique *of* ranking, organized around three meta-choices that precede any quantitative exercise: the choice of algorithm, the choice of variables, and the choice of data. The argument is that each of these choices carries substantive implications for the resulting ranking distribution, and that intellectual capital — Human, Structural, Relational — is particularly vulnerable to such choices precisely because its dimensions are not directly observable.

The empirical illustration, again on Italian universities, compares ranking distributions obtained from several efficiency and multi-criteria methods, demonstrating that the *implementation problem* in performance measurement is not a matter of insufficient technique but of unavoidable subjectivity when many evaluation criteria coexist. The proposed response — *technologies of humility*, and the acceptance of *satisficing* rather than optimizing — is intellectually serious and somewhat countercultural in a field that often treats benchmarks as if they were facts of nature.

This meta-theoretical commitment is not abandoned when the work moves to concrete ranking exercises; it conditions them. "A heuristic approach based on Leiden rankings to identify outliers" (2023), with Daraio and Loet Leydesdorff, frames the use of Leiden Rankings as a *first-pass heuristic* — explicitly non-rigorous, explicitly biased by Web of Science coverage, but useful for flagging institutional profiles that warrant deeper investigation. The honest framing is the point: outliers are to be analyzed as case studies, not penalized or celebrated on the strength of a single instrument.

## Efficiency Analysis as Realistic Target-Setting

The bulk of Di Leo's empirical work sits inside the non-parametric efficiency tradition, but the methodological signature is consistent: realism over abstraction, conditioning over assumption, and explicit attention to the heterogeneity that conventional DEA tends to suppress.

"A Flexible and Sustainable Analysis of Waste Efficiency at the European Level" (2024), with D'Adamo, Daraio, and Simar, applies directional DEA at the country level with waste generated as input, landfilled and incinerated waste as bad output, and recycled waste as good output. Country-level social and economic heterogeneity is built directly into the targets, producing five distinct performance groups rather than a single league table. "Impact of a regulatory target and external factors on the waste efficiency of Italian municipalities" (2025), with Daraio and Simar, brings the same logic to the micro level: 275 Italian municipalities over 2016–2019, against the 65% sorted-collection target of Legislative Decree 152/2006. The non-parametric estimation reveals that municipalities meeting the target are more cost-efficient as costs rise, and identifies population and urban economic development as decisive conditioning factors — with implications for the design of place-specific policy measures such as the proposed *neighbourhood solidarity*.

"Viable eco-efficiency targets for waste collection communities" (2024), with Daraio and Simar, extends the conditioning logic to ATO-level organization. The framing is again explicit: targets are *viable* only when they benchmark each municipality against others with similar external contexts, so that the path to the eco-efficiency frontier is realistic and actionable. "Driving EU sustainability: Promoting the circular economy through municipal waste efficiency" (2024), with D'Adamo, Daraio, Gastaldi, and Rossi, couples a systematic literature review — refined from 216 to 31 articles through double screening — with a standard DEA across the EU-27 over 2017–2021, distinguishing economic, technical, and sustainable models, and surfacing the fragmented geography of European circular performance.

The technical-methodological core of this strand is "Conical Free Disposal Hull estimators of directional distances and Luenberger productivity indices for general technologies" (2025), with Daraio and Simar. The contribution is to extend directional distance measurement to the *cone spanned by the attainable set* — necessary for defining Luenberger indices under general (non-convex) technologies. The result is a method that allows inference on productivity indices and their decompositions without imposing convexity, accompanied by simulated data and a real-data application. This is the foundation on which the applied country- and municipality-level work rests, and it represents Di Leo's most sustained engagement with the formal apparatus of efficiency measurement.

The same efficiency lens is applied, with different instruments, to retail energy. "A robust benchmarking of direct margin in Italy's energy retail markets" (2024), with Chicca, Daraio, Guerrini, and Scarcella, exploits the post-2016 Italian separation of retail activities to isolate direct-margin performance among 120 operators in 2020, finding a positive effect of size on efficiency. "A Framework for the Analysis of the Sustainability of the Energy Retail Market" (2022), with Chicca, Daraio, Guerrini, and Scarcella, complements the quantitative benchmarking with a systematic review and bibliometric analysis of 48 papers, producing a conceptual framework for evaluating technical, economic, and environmental impacts in retail energy policy.

## The Energy Transition as Socio-Technical System

A second major arc moves the focus from efficiency benchmarking to the financial engineering and behavioural analysis of the energy transition. Here Di Leo's collaborators include D'Adamo and Gastaldi as recurring partners, and the methodological signature is integration: financial indicators, sensitivity analysis, scenario analysis, Monte Carlo simulation, and survey-based behavioural analysis are combined in ways that resist disciplinary boundary work.

The financial-engineering core is densely developed. "Green restaurants: An economic assessment of solar photovoltaics and energy storage systems" (2026), with D'Adamo, Gastaldi, and Maccallini, evaluates PV and BES profitability in the catering sector under Italian policy conditions, using NPV, DCF, LASSO regression, break-even analysis, and Monte Carlo simulation. The result is a sharp, policy-sensitive finding: PV is profitable in both incentive and no-incentive contexts, but incentives shift returns from 425 to 1590 €/kW, while BES profitability requires a self-consumption increase of 22–25% with incentives and 30–35% without. "Advancing Sustainable Development Through Integrated Photovoltaic and Battery Energy Storage Systems in Commercial Buildings" (2025), with Antonini, D'Adamo, Gastaldi, and Scarcelli, applies MCDA, DCF, and emission-reduction quantification to commercial buildings, with experts recommending a 50% tax deduction. "Economic assessment of battery energy storage in industrial applications" (2026), with D'Adamo, Fabbi, Gastaldi, and Kalogirou, evaluates a 1500 kWh BES integrated with 1 MW PV at a pharmaceutical plant, finding that profitability requires the simultaneous alignment of public support, low CAPEX, and substantial self-consumption enhancement. "Towards SDG 7: A Quantitative Sustainability Framework for Evaluating PV+BES Systems in the Low-Carbon Transition" (2026), with D'Adamo, Gastaldi, and Scarcelli, adds greenhouse gas emission reduction value to the NPV/LASSO framework, using a Rome shopping mall as case study. "A policy-oriented framework for assessing the economic sustainability of industrial photovoltaic investments" (2026), with D'Adamo, Fabbi, and Gastaldi, demonstrates a 1 MW industrial PV case where a 45% capital grant raises NPV from 1190 to 1610 €/kW and reduces discounted payback from over seven to under two years, with monetized CO2 avoidance of approximately 37,000–63,000 €.

"Exploring the economic feasibility of offshore wind energy for sustainable development" (2025), with D'Adamo, Gastaldi, Ozturk, and Terzi di Bergamo, broadens the technological perimeter: NPV ranges from 195 to 1324 €/kW, IRR 9–15%, LCOE 125–138 €/MWh, with selling price, energy inflation rate, and capacity factor as the key drivers. Across these studies, the contribution is not a single number but a *systematic quantitative framework* for evaluating renewable self-consumption investments under realistic policy and market uncertainty.

The behavioural counterpart is "Energy policy and citizens' perceptions: The role of PV+BES systems in driving a sustainable energy transition" (2026), with D'Adamo, Fabbi, Gastaldi, and Uricchio. Using survey data on Italian citizens, the study finds PV investment rated 8.75/10 as a sustainability priority, PV+BES 7.90/10, with 55% of respondents unable to modify their consumption habits — and thus a structural need for BES to enable self-consumption. Self-consumption outranks incentives in citizen priorities, and cluster analysis identifies the profiles most receptive to industrial-context adoption. The implication is that the energy transition is not primarily a financial-engineering problem but a socio-technical one in which household habits, demographic composition, and perception of investment priority are co-equal with policy design.

## Heterogeneity, Segmentation, and the Circular Consumer

The behavioural and segmentation logic developed for energy citizens extends, in Di Leo's work, to two circular-economy domains where consumer heterogeneity is the central challenge.

"From Awareness to Action: Consumer Behaviour, Attitudes and Business Strategy in Sustainable and Circular Electronic Devices" (2026), with D'Adamo, De Meester, Gastaldi, and Lunadei, analyses 407 Italian respondents through statistical tests, PCA, and K-means clustering. The findings are precise: good environmental awareness does not consistently translate into behaviour; only 29.48% of consumers know where to dispose of end-of-life devices; 53.04% report domestic accumulation; generational patterns distinguish younger (interested in design, less responsible in disposal) from older consumers (more aware, more informed); and economic incentives (30.71%) are the most effective lever. The implication is a differentiated strategy architecture — educational, economic, and premium — rather than a single policy instrument.

"Integrating knowledge management and digital product passports to foster sustainable and collaborative ecosystems" (2026), with Becchi, D'Adamo, Gastaldi, Grosso, and Trusiani, extends the same methodological apparatus — k-means, PCA, logistic regression — to the fashion and cosmetics industries. The Digital Product Passport is reconceptualized as a socio-technical and cognitive infrastructure that embeds open innovation, knowledge management, and circular economy principles. The segmentation distinguishes *aware* consumers (attuned to sustainability and digital innovation) from *unaware* consumers (oriented to immediate tangible benefits), with implementation success depending on alignment between DPP technical capabilities, organizational readiness, and consumer engagement.

## Universities as Objects of Structural and Comparative Analysis

The university as a unit of analysis returns throughout Di Leo's work, but with shifting instruments and shifting questions. Where the meta-choices paper is epistemological and the Leiden-outliers paper is heuristic, two later studies are quantitative-comparative.

"The role of the non-academic staff heterogeneity in the performance of European universities" (2026), with Avenali, Daraio, and Wolszczak-Derlacz, applies a non-parametric approach to 401 European universities under the New Public Management paradigm, treating non-academic staff heterogeneity as an unobserved latent factor. The empirical finding is that the *proportion* of non-academic staff is not statistically significant for university efficiency, but their *unobserved heterogeneity* — capturing qualitative and structural differences beyond size and composition — significantly shifts both the efficient frontier and the inefficiency distribution. Universities with high heterogeneity combined with large administrative shares suffer from bureaucratic overload; universities that effectively integrate diverse professional competences transform heterogeneity into a productive resource. The finding reframes the long-running debate on the university's administrative apparatus.

"Heterogeneity of national accounting systems, world-class universities and financial resources" (2024), with Avenali, Daraio, and Wolszczak-Derlacz, focuses on the relationship between financial resources, the heterogeneous national accounting systems that report them, and the Shanghai Ranking tier. The result is a predictive model linking financial resources to ranking tier, and a stark policy implication: concentrating resources on a small number of already-ranked universities may be a necessary condition for international competitiveness, while proportional distribution does not affect the global ranking game.

"Climbing university rankings under resources constraints" (2025), with Avenali, Daraio, and Wolszczak-Derlacz, brings the methodological apparatus of the efficiency arc to the university ranking problem. By combining DEA with directed Louvain community detection on 318 universities across five countries, the study identifies clusters of universities with similar financial profiles and corresponding ARWU scores, and surfaces benchmarks — universities that have optimized their use of resources — that similar institutions can emulate. The application to Italian universities closes a loop with the earlier Italian-university work, but with a different epistemic posture: the targets are not abstract best-practice frontiers but peer-resolved, resource-constrained possibilities.

## Crossing the Digital Twin Divide: Methodological Bridge-Building

"An Integrated and Flexible Review Framework to Evaluate the Evolution and Barriers of Digital-Twin Technologies in Industrial and Healthcare Domains" (2025), with Daraio and Orsini, develops the Integrated Flexible Systematic Review (IFSR) framework to analyze digital twin adoption across two otherwise siloed sectors. Drawing on 133 rigorously selected articles, the study identifies industrial maturity (predictive maintenance, supply-chain resilience, smart manufacturing) and healthcare emergence (personalized medicine, surgical simulation, patient-specific diagnostics), and — critically — the cross-domain spillovers through which industrial innovations in real-time analytics, AR/VR, and federated learning are beginning to influence healthcare, while healthcare's demands for precision and ethics are reshaping industrial DT practice. The barrier inventory — interoperability gaps, regulatory constraints, computational challenges — is differentiated by sector. The methodological contribution is to demonstrate that flexible system management principles can organize a cross-domain review in a way that single-domain SLRs cannot.

## Composite Indicators and the Construction of Sustainability Rankings

"Evaluating sustainability in Europe with composite indicators" (2025), with D'Adamo, Gastaldi, and Paris, applies min-max normalization and TOPSIS to 76 Eurostat SDG indicators for 2022, comparing equal and differential weighting schemes. The methodological finding — strong consistency between the two methods under equal weighting, with Sweden, Denmark, and the Netherlands at the top and Greece, Cyprus, and Bulgaria at the bottom — is paired with disaggregation by dimension (Germany in environmental, Sweden in social, the Netherlands in economic). The three recommendations — strengthen cohesion, promote integrated strategies, improve data quality — are anchored in the same constructive-skeptical posture that pervades the meta-choices work: composite indicators are powerful but constructed, and their construction is the methodological work that matters.

## Systematic Reviews as Metaknowledge, and AI as a New Instrument

Two final strands address the methodology of synthesis itself. "Systematic reviews as a metaknowledge tool" (2023), with Avenali, Daraio, Matteucci, and Nepomuceno, addresses the exponential growth of scholarly publications and the proliferation of review software tools, analyzing coverage of the journal *International Transactions in Operational Research* to demonstrate that domain expert-based selection and tool-assisted review are complementary rather than substitutable. The argument is that good systematic review relies on the intelligent combination of the latest tools with domain expertise.

"Pitfalls, benefits, and comparative analysis of artificial intelligence ChatBots in the systematic review process" (2026), with Daraio and Ferazzoli, extends this analysis empirically through a case study on the *International Transactions in Operational Research* journal, benchmarking AI chatbots against the manual SLR workflow. The contribution is concrete evidence on retrieval accuracy, methodological compliance, and pitfalls across SLR stages — a study that closes the loop on Di Leo's long-standing interest in the *instruments* of synthesis by examining the newest such instrument with the same evaluative posture.

## Intellectual Trajectory: A Unifying Reading

What holds these eighteen thematic strands together is not a single domain but a coherent methodological disposition. The work begins with the problem of data quality — the recognition that integrated systems must be designed to disclose their own completeness — and proceeds, in successive arcs, to the construction of efficiency frontiers that respect heterogeneity, the financial appraisal of renewable systems under realistic policy and market conditions, the analysis of consumer and citizen heterogeneity in circular and energy contexts, the meta-theoretical scrutiny of benchmarking instruments, the cross-domain synthesis of digital twin literatures, the construction of composite sustainability indicators, and the methodological evaluation of AI-assisted review.

Each arc inherits the commitments of the preceding ones. The data-quality work establishes that the user's interpretive capacity depends on what the system makes visible. The efficiency work applies this disposition to production frontiers, insisting on conditioning variables and non-convex technologies rather than smooth convex approximations. The meta-choices work extends it to the measurement of intellectual capital, where every variable and algorithm choice carries substantive implications. The energy-transition work deploys the same realism in financial engineering, with self-consumption, policy risk, and market volatility treated as structural rather than residual. The consumer-typology work, finally, translates the disposition into the domain of sustainable behaviour, where heterogeneity is not noise but signal.

The result is a research program in which the central tension — quantification under conditions of heterogeneity, and the awareness that every instrument is a construction — is not resolved but continually renewed. Di Leo's contribution is to have demonstrated, across a remarkably broad empirical portfolio, that this tension is not a limitation of the quantitative social sciences but their most productive working condition.
