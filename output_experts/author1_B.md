## Academic Biography

## The Intellectual Arc: Bridging Efficiency, Sustainability, and Knowledge Systems

Professor Simone Di Leo’s research trajectory is unified by a deep engagement with the measurement, optimization, and synthesis of complex systems—whether in energy markets, circular economies, or academic institutions. His work is animated by a central tension: how to reconcile the technical rigor of quantitative assessment with the messy, context-dependent realities of policy, behavior, and institutional dynamics. This tension has driven him to develop and refine methodologies that are at once robust and adaptive, capable of capturing both the efficiency frontiers of operational systems and the nuanced, often hidden, factors that shape their performance.

This stance enables a critical engagement with policy sustainability, particularly in the retail energy sector, where he constructs **conceptual frameworks** that map technical, economic, and environmental impacts of policy decisions. The metaknowledge lens underpins his ability to navigate the exponential growth of electronic publications, transforming raw data into actionable insights for decision-makers.

This thematic arc evolves into a broader preoccupation with **benchmarking and efficiency analysis**, where the tools of metaknowledge—comprehensive identification, synthesis, and state-of-the-art assessments—are operationalized to evaluate the sustainability of complex systems. The transition is not linear but dialectical: the challenges of synthesizing knowledge in one domain (e.g., energy policy) reveal the need for more sophisticated analytical frameworks in others (e.g., waste management, university performance).
