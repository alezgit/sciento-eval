## Thematic Research Areas

### Completeness-Aware Data Integration Infrastructure
Di Leo's contribution to data integration treats the integration layer itself as a quality-bearing object, arguing that multi-purpose, multi-national systems must be designed so that completeness information is available by construction rather than recovered by the final user. The position is operationalised in a completeness-aware integration approach for research infrastructures and in an integrated Italian-university dataset that reconciles administrative, balance-sheet, and Scopus-bibliometric sources. The work functions as the empirical substrate on which subsequent efficiency, ranking, and meta-choice analyses are conducted.

**Keywords**: completeness-aware integration approach, data quality information at integration level, multi-national historical data integration, knowledge infrastructure, multi-purpose data infrastructure usability

**Key Publications**:

- An integrated dataset providing a landscape of the Italian Universities (2023)

### Digital Product Passports for Circular Consumer Ecosystems

Di Leo and collaborators apply the framework to electronics, fashion, and cosmetics, documenting disposal-knowledge gaps, refurbishment-versus-second-hand market preferences, and the dependence of DPP adoption success on aligning technical capabilities with consumer readiness and SDG 12-aligned incentives.

**Keywords**: digital product passport (dpp), circular and collaborative ecosystems, consumer segmentation, generational cohort, knowledge management

## Academic Biography

# Academic Narrative CV: Professor Simone Di Leo

## Architectures of Measurement: Bridging Data Quality, Efficiency Frontiers, and Sustainability Transitions

Simone Di Leo's research program is organised around a recurring intellectual wager: that the credibility of any quantitative assessment — whether of a knowledge-based organisation, a waste-collection community, an electricity retailer, or a renewable-energy investment — depends on the integrity of the underlying data infrastructure and the methodological honesty of the estimator used to interrogate it. Across two interconnected strands of work, one anchored in nonparametric efficiency and productivity analysis and the other in the economics of sustainable energy and circular systems, Di Leo has consistently pursued what may be called a measurement-first agenda. The wager is methodological rather than rhetorical: rather than treat data quality as a preprocessing nuisance or efficiency estimation as a mechanical exercise, his work insists that the spatial and temporal completeness of integrated data, the heterogeneity of decision-making units, and the policy context conditioning real-world performance are constitutive features of the analytical object itself. This commitment is visible in his sustained attention to data integration, error diagnostics in longitudinal records, and the formal properties of production-frontier estimators, as well as in his turn, more recently, toward the appraisal of photovoltaic and battery storage investments where data, policy design, and financial viability are similarly entangled.

The earliest manifestations of this stance appear in the data-infrastructure work, where Di Leo and collaborators argue that multi-national and historical integration systems must be designed so that the final user accesses an integration layer that is, by design, as complete as possible — a position formalised in 'Accounting for quality in data integration systems: a completeness-aware integration approach' (2022). The same conviction reappears, in a different register, in 'An integrated dataset providing a landscape of the Italian Universities' (2023), where heterogeneous administrative, balance-sheet, and Scopus-bibliometric sources are reconciled into a multi-perspective dataset with explicit attention to missing-value imputation and research-keyword extraction. These data-engineering contributions are not peripheral to the efficiency and ranking work that follows; they are its precondition, supplying the cleaned Italian-university substrate on which several benchmarking and meta-choice analyses are subsequently performed.

## Flexible Frontiers: Nonparametric Efficiency, Error Diagnostics, and the Conditional Estimation of Performance

A second arc of Di Leo's research develops the formal and statistical machinery needed to measure productivity and efficiency under realistic assumptions about technology and heterogeneity. The work on conical Free Disposal Hull estimators of directional distances and Luenberger productivity indices, reported in 'Conical Free Disposal Hull estimators of directional distances and Luenberger productivity indices for general technologies' (2025), extends the directional-distance framework to non-convex attainable sets without imposing Constant Returns to Scale, and provides inference procedures for Luenberger indices and their decompositions. In parallel, 'Flexible Techniques to Detect Typical Hidden Errors in Large Longitudinal Datasets' (2024) proposes a domain-agnostic statistical-mathematical system for identifying elusive errors — trends, fluctuations, and surviving anomalies — that generic correction routines leave behind. Read together, these two contributions share a deep methodological kinship: both treat measurement as a formal problem in which the geometry of the attainable set, in one case, and the structure of consecutive differences in time series, in the other, must be analysed before any substantive performance statement can be defended.

This formal apparatus is then operationalised across multiple empirical settings. In 'A Flexible and Sustainable Analysis of Waste Efficiency at the European Level' (2024), directional Data Envelopment Analysis is applied to European Union countries treating waste generated as input and landfilled/incinerated waste as bad output, with heterogeneity captured through country-specific external factors and realistic targets derived accordingly. 'Impact of a regulatory target and external factors on the waste efficiency of Italian municipalities' (2025) uses nonparametric estimation to evaluate the cost effects of the 65 percent sorted-collection target of Italian Legislative Decree 152/2006 across 275 municipalities, while 'Viable eco-efficiency targets for waste collection communities' (2024) embeds conditional efficiency analysis within ATO-level benchmarking to ensure that viable targets reflect community-specific external factors. The university sector receives the same treatment: 'The role of the non-academic staff heterogeneity in the performance of European universities' (2026) uses a nonparametric approach to recover unobserved heterogeneity of administrative and technical personnel and shows that this latent factor — rather than its mere proportion — drives variation in efficiency, with implications for bureaucratic overload and professional-competence integration. Across these studies, the methodological throughline is the insistence that unobserved heterogeneity, community-specific context, and bad-output treatment are not auxiliary complications but central objects of estimation.

## Benchmarking Knowledge-Based Organisations: Meta-Choices, Indicators, and Ranking Verification

Where the efficiency-frontier work establishes the statistical ground, a parallel strand examines the institutional, indicator-construction, and ranking-verification problems that condition the practical use of efficiency scores in higher-education and policy settings. In 'Meta-choices in ranking knowledge-based organizations' (2022), Di Leo and co-authors identify three meta-choices — algorithm, variables, and data — that determine the apparent ranking of universities and argue for the adoption of what they term technologies of humility, recognising that multidimensional benchmarking can yield only satisficing results. The intellectual capital that any university benchmark depends on is then enumerated in 'DEA, balanced scorecard and intellectual capital including the gender dimension: A comprehensive list of indicators' (2023), which combines systematic reviews, bibliometric analysis, and grey-literature review to produce an inclusive indicator list classified along Human, Structural, and Relational capital dimensions.

The Leiden Ranking outlier-detection work, 'A heuristic approach based on Leiden rankings to identify outliers: evidence from Italian universities in the European landscape' (2023), reframes ranking analysis as a verification problem: rather than read rankings as league tables, the authors use statistical analysis and network mapping to identify outlier institutions whose research output departs from expectation, illustrating their method through the case of the Politecnico di Bari and arguing that small teams led by young scholars may lift institutional performance to the top tier. The financial determinants of ranking position are addressed in 'Heterogeneity of national accounting systems, world-class universities and financial resources: What are the links?' (2024), which establishes a strong relationship between total available financial resources and position in the Shanghai Ranking's top tier and warns that a proportional distribution of resources may be inefficient under international competition. The same logic is taken a step further in 'Climbing university rankings under resources constraints: a combined approach integrating DEA and directed Louvain community detection' (2025), where Data Envelopment Analysis is combined with a directed Louvain community detection algorithm over 318 universities to identify clusters sharing financial profiles and corresponding ARWU scores, isolating benchmarks that can be emulated by resource-constrained institutions.

## The Knowledge-Synthesis Apparatus: Systematic Reviews, AI Chatbots, and Cross-Sector Digital Twins

A more meta-level concern animates Di Leo's methodological writing on the systematic review itself, treated here as a metaknowledge tool rather than a mere literature survey. 'Systematic reviews as a metaknowledge tool: caveats and a review of available options' (2023) documents the proliferation of literature reviews driven by the exponential growth of electronic scholarly publications and argues that good systematic reviews rely on the intelligent combination of contemporary tools with domain-expert knowledge. The companion study, 'Pitfalls, benefits, and comparative analysis of artificial intelligence ChatBots in the systematic review process' (2026), compares AI ChatBots leveraging natural language processing for information retrieval and knowledge synthesis against traditional manual approaches using a case study focused on the International Transactions in Operational Research journal, thereby generating empirical evidence on the practical implications of AI-assisted SLRs. The cross-sectoral reach of this review apparatus is illustrated in 'An Integrated and Flexible Review Framework to Evaluate the Evolution and Barriers of Digital-Twin Technologies in Industrial and Healthcare Domains' (2025), which applies an Integrated Flexible Systematic Review to 133 articles and identifies interoperability gaps, regulatory constraints, and computational challenges as cross-sectoral adoption barriers rather than domain-specific ones.

## Sustainable Systems in Practice: Circular Waste, Retail Markets, and Composite SDG Indicators

The methodological commitments developed in the efficiency-and-ranking strand are continuously re-applied in substantive sustainability settings. 'Driving EU sustainability: Promoting the circular economy through municipal waste efficiency' (2024) presents a systematic literature review of waste-management efficiency and a standard Data Envelopment Analysis of 27 European countries over 2017–2021, optimising municipal-waste recycling and circular material use through three models and finding a stronger synergy between technical and sustainability dimensions than between economic and sustainability components. At the indicator level, 'Evaluating sustainability in Europe with composite indicators' (2025) employs min-max normalisation and TOPSIS on the 76 Eurostat SDG indicators, equal-weighted across economic, environmental, and social dimensions, and positions the resulting country-level rankings as inputs to European cohesion policy rather than as a generic scoring exercise, highlighting the consistency of the two methodologies and the persistent performance disparities across member states. The energy-retail counterpart is 'A robust benchmarking of direct margin in Italy's energy retail markets' (2024), which uses two models to assess cost and commercial efficiency of 120 Italian electricity and gas retailers under the structural conditions produced by market liberalisation in the 2000s and retail unbundling in 2016, and the earlier 'A Framework for the Analysis of the Sustainability of the Energy Retail Market' (2022) constructs a conceptual framework for retail-energy policy sustainability analysis that integrates technical, economic, and environmental impact dimensions.

## Renewable Investment Appraisal: PV, BES, and Offshore Wind under Policy Uncertainty

Di Leo's most recent body of work turns explicitly toward the economic appraisal of renewable-energy investments under policy uncertainty, where the methodological apparatus of discounted cash flow analysis, sensitivity testing, and Monte Carlo simulation is brought to bear on photovoltaic, battery-storage, and offshore-wind deployment. The restaurant-sector assessment in 'Green restaurants: An economic assessment of solar photovoltaics and energy storage systems' (2026) combines NPV analysis, LASSO regression, scenario and break-even analysis, and Monte Carlo simulation to show that PV profitability depends on specific production, electricity-purchase cost, and the self-consumption rate, while BES profitability requires self-consumption increases of 22–25 percent with incentives and 30–35 percent without them. The industrial-scale counterpart in 'Economic assessment of battery energy storage in industrial applications: Policy support and self-consumption effects' (2026) evaluates a 1500 kWh BES integrated with a 1 MW PV system at a pharmaceutical plant and finds that break-even self-consumption decreases from 44 percent without capital grants to 37 percent with a capital contribution and 31 percent under favourable energy purchase prices, with Monte Carlo simulation confirming the dominant role of self-consumption over equivalent CAPEX reductions. The policy-resilience framing is consolidated in 'A policy-oriented framework for assessing the economic sustainability of industrial photovoltaic investments' (2026), which demonstrates that a 45 percent capital grant raises NPV from 1190 €/kW to 1610 €/kW, reduces the discounted payback period from more than seven years to less than two, and monetises avoided CO2 emissions at approximately 37,000–63,000 €. The commercial-building and SDG-7 perspective is provided by 'Advancing Sustainable Development Through Integrated Photovoltaic and Battery Energy Storage Systems in Commercial Buildings: A Strategic, Economic, and Environmental Perspective' (2025) and 'Towards SDG 7: A Quantitative Sustainability Framework for Evaluating PV+BES Systems in the Low-Carbon Transition' (2026), the latter using NPV, sensitivity, and LASSO regression over a Rome shopping mall to disentangle the effects of self-consumption, electricity purchase price, and the composition of the fossil-fuel mix replaced. Offshore wind is examined under a multi-scenario risk lens in 'Exploring the economic feasibility of offshore wind energy for sustainable development' (2025), which combines NPV, IRR, Profitability Index, Discounted Payback Time, and LCOE across multiple market scenarios and capacity factors, integrating national energy independence and resilience with environmental protection objectives.

## Citizen Engagement and Digital Product Passports: Behavioural Antecedents of the Sustainability Transition

The renewable-energy work is complemented by attention to the behavioural and informational conditions under which sustainability transitions actually take hold among citizens and consumers. 'Energy policy and citizens' perceptions: The role of PV+BES systems in driving a sustainable energy transition' (2026) draws on an Italian online survey to profile receptive citizens through demographic segmentation (gender, age above thirty) and cluster analysis, finding that PV is rated 8.75/10 as a sustainable-future priority while PV+BES integration receives 7.90/10, and that self-consumption is valued more than government incentives. On the circular-economy and consumer side, 'From Awareness to Action: Consumer Behaviour, Attitudes and Business Strategy in Sustainable and Circular Electronic Devices' (2026) combines statistical tests, principal component analysis, and K-means clustering on a 407-respondent sample to map generational and gender differences in the purchase, replacement, and end-of-life management of electronic devices, documenting a domestic e-waste accumulation rate of 53.04 percent and identifying economic incentives as the most effective lever for sustainable behaviour. The architectural counterpart is 'Integrating knowledge management and digital product passports to foster sustainable and collaborative ecosystems' (2026), which treats the Digital Product Passport as a socio-technical and cognitive infrastructure that integrates knowledge management, open innovation, and circular-economy principles across the fashion and cosmetics value chains and segments Italian consumers into aware and unaware clusters via K-means and principal component analysis.

## Integrative Horizons: Toward Conditional, Heterogeneity-Aware Sustainability Measurement

Across these thematic arcs, Di Leo's research programme traces a coherent trajectory from data quality and longitudinal-error diagnostics, through nonparametric efficiency estimation under conditional and non-convex technologies, to the empirical benchmarking of waste, university, and energy-retail systems and the economic appraisal of renewable-energy investments under policy uncertainty. The intellectual continuity is not merely thematic but methodological: the completeness-aware integration logic that underpins the Italian-university dataset feeds directly into the heterogeneity-aware efficiency analyses of European waste collection and higher education; the directional-distance and Luenberger-index estimators developed for general technologies provide the formal scaffolding for the waste and university work; the meta-choice and technologies-of-humility framing conditions the practical interpretation of every ranking exercise; and the systematic-review apparatus developed for the operational-research literature is re-deployed to study digital twins, AI chatbots, and the cross-sectoral dynamics of knowledge synthesis. The forward horizon implied by this trajectory is one in which sustainability measurement becomes increasingly conditional, multidimensional, and heterogeneity-aware — moving from league-table ranking toward verified, context-sensitive performance diagnostics, and from generic cost-benefit analysis toward the explicit joint appraisal of economic, environmental, and social returns under realistic policy and data conditions.
