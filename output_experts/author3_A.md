# Academic Narrative CV: Professor Idiano D'Adamo

## Quantifying the Sustainability Transition: Techno-Economic Architectures and Social Value

Professor Idiano D'Adamo has established a comprehensive research program centered on the quantitative modeling, economic feasibility, and socio-institutional governance of the global ecological transition. Operating at the intersection of industrial engineering, techno-economic analysis, decision science, and public policy, his work addresses the fundamental challenge of aligning technological innovation with economic viability and social equity. His scholarship systematically bridges empirical quantitative assessments—such as discounted cash flow modeling, multi-criteria decision analysis (MCDA), and life-cycle characterization—with behavioral economics and institutional policy design across sectors including renewable energy systems, circular waste recovery, corporate environmental strategy, and territorial development goals.

Through rigorous empirical assessments of decentralized energy systems, critical mineral supply chains, digital product passports, and macro-regional sustainability indicators, Professor D'Adamo's research demonstrates that technological deployment cannot achieve systemic decarbonization without robust business models, targeted regulatory incentives, and deep public engagement. His intellectual trajectory moves seamlessly from hyper-local technical configurations, such as building-integrated photovoltaics and waste-to-energy systems, to global supply chain geopolitics and composite SDG monitoring frameworks, establishing an integrated paradigm for pragmatic, actionable sustainability.

## Administrative Capacities and Equity in Renewable Energy Communities

Central to Professor D'Adamo's research program is the examination of decentralized energy governance, specifically focusing on how institutional capacity and targeted incentive structures catalyze local energy transitions while mitigating energy poverty. In 'Public administration as a driver of sustainability within energy communities' (2026), he evaluates how municipal leadership and public-community synergies serve as essential enablers of citizen participation and prosumer emergence, arguing that integrating technical-scientific expertise into public administrations overcomes administrative bottlenecks. This focus on local energy governance extends into behavioral dynamics in 'Energy Communities Toward Sustainable Development: The Role of Economic Factors in a Social Analysis' (2025) and 'Motivational and Behavioural Determinants of Participation in Renewable Energy Communities' (2026), where empirical survey modeling reveals that while environmental concern drives initial interest, financial parameters—including energy purchase, sale, and exchange prices—dictate sustained prosumer engagement, with behavioral nudges and framing strategies significantly altering stated willingness to participate.

To bridge economic performance with social equity, Professor D'Adamo developed advanced software-based flow models to design targeted benefit-sharing structures. In 'Renewable energy communities and energy poverty: A novel benefit-sharing model based on differentiated pricing and incentive allocation' (2026), he utilizes the custom S-REC Python software to demonstrate that dynamic revenue redistribution and differentiated tariff structures can drop discounted payback periods to two years while successfully lowering energy expenses for vulnerable households below critical energy poverty thresholds. Complementary insights in 'Fostering sustainable economic development and mitigating energy poverty through renewable energy communities' (2026), 'Renewable energy communities for sustainable cities: Economic insights into subsidies, market dynamics and benefits distribution' (2025), and 'Socioeconomic dimensions of renewable energy communities: Pathways to collective well-being' (2025) validate that photovoltaic-based energy communities yield positive net present values even without public subsidies, though structured capital grants and profit-sharing models prioritizing energy equity maximize community legitimacy and collective well-being.

## Techno-Economic Assessment of Clean Technology Innovation

Professor D'Adamo has advanced the field of techno-economic modeling by integrating granular financial metrics with risk, sensitivity, and scenario analyses across diverse low-carbon technologies. His evaluations of integrated solar photovoltaic (PV) and battery energy storage (BES) systems establish clear economic boundaries for self-consumption. In 'Economic assessment of battery energy storage in industrial applications: Policy support and self-consumption effects' (2026), 'A policy-oriented framework for assessing the economic sustainability of industrial photovoltaic investments' (2026), and 'Sustainability and competitiveness: Economic analysis of a photovoltaic plant with storage in an Italian textile company' (2025), he combines net present value formulations with Monte Carlo simulations to show that industrial BES integration requires a critical threshold increase in self-consumption—typically between thirty and forty percent—alongside capital grant support to maintain financial feasibility against electricity price volatility.

This rigorous quantitative framework extends across multiple commercial, residential, and emerging clean technology applications. In 'Advancing Sustainable Development Through Integrated Photovoltaic and Battery Energy Storage Systems in Commercial Buildings: A Strategic, Economic, and Environmental Perspective' (2025), 'Towards SDG 7: A Quantitative Sustainability Framework for Evaluating PV+BES Systems in the Low-Carbon Transition' (2026), 'A distributed and sustainable model for future cities: A profitability analysis of integrated photovoltaic systems with storage under different incentive policies' (2025), 'Green restaurants: An economic assessment of solar photovoltaics and energy storage systems' (2026), and 'Energy policy and citizens’ perceptions: The role of PV+BES systems in driving a sustainable energy transition' (2026), he applies LASSO regression and discounted cash flow modeling to identify that avoided electricity costs and tax deduction policies serve as primary financial catalysts for urban prosumers. Beyond solar infrastructure, his techno-economic evaluations encompass alternative decarbonization pathways, including offshore wind farms in 'Exploring the economic feasibility of offshore wind energy for sustainable development' (2025), green hydrogen production via wind-electrolysis in 'Green hydrogen as a sustainable operations strategy: A socio-economic perspective' (2025), ocean alkalinity enhancement in 'Economic Assessment of Ocean Alkalinity Enhancement Through Electrodialysis: The Role of Carbon Credits in Comparing Solar Energy and Thermal Waste Technologies' (2026), and urban organic waste valorization in 'Social acceptance and economic impacts of biomethane: A resource for energy sustainability' (2025) and 'The Economics and Policy of Biomethane: A Pragmatic Approach to Renewable Energy and Waste Valorization' (2025).

In 'Sustainable development at the crossroads: Navigating eco-humanism and eco-modernism' (2025), he articulates a theoretical critique of eco-modernist technological optimism, highlighting the socio-ethical friction that arises when high-tech, top-down decarbonization initiatives bypass community consultation and local equity considerations. He proposes an integrative eco-humanist framework that couples advanced technical solutions with human-centered ethics, interdisciplinary pedagogy, and inclusive participatory governance, ensuring that technological progress remains anchored in social justice.

## Multi-Criteria Evaluation of Global, European, and Regional SDGs

To evaluate macro-level sustainability progress across political boundaries, Professor D'Adamo specializes in multi-criteria decision analysis (MCDA) and composite indicator construction. In 'Indicator Assessment of Sustainable Development Goals: A Global Perspective' (2025) and 'A Quantitative Analysis and Interdisciplinary Approach to the Sustainable Development Goals' (2026), he applies comparative aggregation methodologies—specifically min-max normalization and the Technique for Order of Preference by Similarity to Ideal Solution (TOPSIS)—across global datasets, revealing the operational sensitivity of national SDG rankings to aggregation mathematics while evaluating performance across European, OECD, and BRICS+ economies.

At the macro-regional level, 'Evaluating sustainability in Europe with composite indicators' (2025) leverages Eurostat indicators to benchmark European Union member states, identifying key structural disparities and underscoring the necessity of targeted cohesion policies. ## Advanced Spectroscopic Characterization and Space Waste Recovery

Extending circular economy principles to highly technical waste flows, Professor D'Adamo's research addresses advanced characterization, automated sorting, and industrial waste recovery architectures. Understanding the demand side of the circular economy, Professor D'Adamo utilizes experimental choice designs and survey econometrics to analyze consumer preferences, price premiums, and digital transparency tools. In 'Environmental Taxes and Subsidies for Sustainability: Experimental Evidence on Consumer Preferences for Business Strategy' (2026), he conducts experimental pricing studies in fast fashion, demonstrating that direct economic subsidies are significantly more effective than carbon taxes in driving consumer adoption of sustainable textiles, establishing a measurable 'circular premium'. This focus on circular textiles is expanded in 'Determinants of consumers’ hypothetical willingness to pay for circular fashion' (2026), 'How consumer shopping habits affect willingness to embrace sustainable fashion' (2025), and 'Advancing Business Strategy in End-Of-Life Management for the Fashion Industry' (2025), where multi-criteria evaluation of end-of-life textile options shows that while consumers exhibit an attitude-behavior gap toward second-hand garments, targeted environmental education and reuse strategies outperform mechanical recycling and landfilling.

To bridge information asymmetries between producers and consumers, Professor D'Adamo investigates the strategic implementation of Digital Product Passports (DPPs) across consumer sectors. In 'Fashion, transparency, and sustainability: the role of the digital product passport' (2026), 'Environmental Concerns in the Fashion Industry: A Twin Transition With the Digital Product Passport' (2025), 'An Integrated Business Strategy for the Twin Transition: Leveraging Digital Product Passports and Circular Economy Models' (2025), 'Digital product passports for cleaner production: Economic evidence from producers and consumers in the fashion industry' (2026), and 'Integrating knowledge management and digital product passports to foster sustainable and collaborative ecosystems' (2026), he combines AHP and TOPSIS frameworks to show how DPPs enhance material traceability, repairability, and supply chain transparency in textiles. Extending this digital strategy to personal care and electronics, 'Bridging digital tools and human skills: A sustainable competence framework for implementing digital product passports in the cosmetics industry' (2026) and 'From Awareness to Action: Consumer Behaviour, Attitudes and Business Strategy in Sustainable and Circular Electronic Devices' (2026) highlight how DPP-enabled data governance fosters consumer brand trust and guides responsible e-waste disposal, provided companies invest in both human competence frameworks and digital infrastructure.

## Geopolitics, Supply Chain Resilience, and Critical Mineral Management

In the realm of macro-economic resource management, Professor D'Adamo addresses the geopolitical, financial, and environmental dimensions of primary metal supply chains and end-of-life processing. In 'A Flexible and Circular Management of Copper in Chile: New Perspectives Toward Sustainable Development' (2025), he uses advanced supply chain simulations to show that expanding domestic refining capacity in primary copper-exporting nations reduces international transport carbon emissions by up to fifteen percent while insulating domestic GDP from export shocks and geopolitical instability. Expanding this international infrastructure planning to regional transport corridors, 'The Lobito corridor: A catalyst for international Cooperation, regional Growth, and sustainable supply chain development' (2026) assesses critical raw material corridor development across sub-Saharan Africa, emphasizing coordinated infrastructure investment to transition mining hubs from passive exporters to active circular value-chain hubs.

His research on industrial recycling processes combines high-value material extraction with macroeconomic modeling. In 'Circular economy and profitability: A techno-economic analysis of precious metal recovery from waste printed circuit boards' (2026), he performs a detailed cash-flow and sensitivity analysis on a patented hydro-metallurgical process for processing waste printed circuit boards, proving that precious metal recovery—specifically gold—yields substantial net present values even under volatile market conditions. In the automotive domain, 'A review on European sustainable practices in end-of-life vehicles management' (2025) links national gross domestic product per capita with end-of-life vehicle recycling rates across twenty-seven European nations, identifying structural economic clusters above €35,000 per capita that drive advanced vehicle treatment adoption.

## Macro-Regional Investment Impact and Territorial Divide Mitigation

Looking toward the future horizon of sustainability science, Professor D'Adamo's research program is positioning itself to synthesize micro-level technical systems with macro-level policy architectures. His ongoing trajectory focuses on developing adaptive, data-driven frameworks that unite artificial intelligence, digital product passports, dynamic energy community modeling, and closed-loop material recovery systems. By continually expanding his quantitative methodologies to encompass emerging challenges—from extra-terrestrial circular economies to critical mineral supply chain security—his work establishes a cohesive roadmap for industrial decarbonization.

Through his academic contributions, 'Sustainable communities: we are the world' (2025), 'Strategy and Development in Pharmacy Holding Companies: Enhancing Sustainable Lifestyle and Unlocking New Business Opportunities' (2025), and 'Management of pharmacy holdings and consumer behaviour: entrepreneurship strategies for sustainable ecosystems' (2026), Professor D'Adamo consistently reinforces that achieving true sustainability demands a holistic alignment of economic profitability, regulatory leadership, and collective social well-being, positioning his research at the vanguard of the global green transition.
