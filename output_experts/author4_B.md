## Epistemic Integrity and Frontier Econometrics: The Reconstruction of Evaluative Systems

The academic trajectory of Professor Cinzia Daraio is defined by a sustained methodological inquiry into the measurement, governance, and epistemological limits of organizational performance. Operating across the boundaries of operational research, econometrics, and science and technology studies, Daraio has systematically investigated the reductionist assumptions inherent in standard efficiency metrics and university league tables. Her research program directly interrogates the structural friction between technical optimization and ethical evaluation. Rather than treating evaluative indicators as passive mirrors of institutional reality, her corpus approaches measurement as an active, value-laden intervention that alters behavior, shapes incentives, and requires rigorous mathematical and philosophical grounding. Through the dual development of nonparametric frontier analysis and virtue-based evaluation frameworks, Daraio has constructed an integrated agenda that bridges advanced production theory with the responsible governance of knowledge-intensive systems.

Central to this intellectual project is the realization that technical precision in efficiency estimation cannot be decoupled from the data infrastructures and normative commitments that underpin it. When evaluating complex non-market public institutions, such as higher education systems, police departments, or municipal public services, conventional econometric models frequently rely on convenient but empirically restrictive assumptions of convexity, perfect compensation, and input homogeneity. Daraio’s work demonstrates how unmeasured qualitative dimensions, structural data corruption, and unexamined policy heuristics distort performance assessments. Her scholarship embodies an ongoing dialectic between developing flexible, non-compensatory mathematical estimators capable of handling real-world operational trade-offs and formulating philosophical frameworks that preserve epistemic integrity in audit cultures.

## Methodological Foundations: Nonparametric Frontiers, Non-Convex Technologies, and Outranking Structures

The mathematical core of Daraio’s research program addresses structural limitations within Data Envelopment Analysis (DEA) and productive efficiency measurement. A persistent line of inquiry investigates how traditional convex hulls impose artificial substitution trade-offs between conflicting organizational goals. To map empirical boundaries of technical performance without imposing arbitrary parametric restrictions, Daraio developed flexible frontier approximations that relax conventional convexity axioms. In 'Approximations and inference for envelopment estimators of production frontiers' (2024), Daraio and Simar formulated local linear smoothing models to derive marginal products, economic coefficients, and partial derivatives directly from nonparametrically smoothed boundaries, establishing bootstrap-based statistical inference without distributional assumptions. This line of frontier estimation was extended into non-convex settings in 'Conical Free Disposal Hull estimators of directional distances and Luenberger productivity indices for general technologies' (2025), where Daraio, Di Leo, and Simar resolved the theoretical challenge of projecting decision-making units onto the conical hull of non-convex attainable sets, formulating Luenberger productivity indices and their structural decompositions under constant returns to scale.

Recognizing that traditional frontier estimation frequently allows superior performance along one operational axis to compensate mathematically for severe deficiencies along another, Daraio introduced outranking multi-criteria decision analysis into production theory. In 'Combining Directional Distances and ELECTRE Multicriteria Decision Analysis for Preferable Assessments of Efficiency' (2023), Nepomuceno and Daraio coupled directional distance vectors with the ELECTRE outranking formalism, establishing non-compensatory preference structures that prevent the arbitrary trade-off of bad outputs, non-discretionary inputs, and undesirable operating configurations. This methodological integration expanded in 'Multicriteria panel-data directional distances and the efficiency measurement of multidimensional higher education systems' (2024), where Nepomuceno, Agasisti, Bertoletti, and Daraio utilized outranking logic to structure output expansion vectors across teaching, research, and third-mission activities, reflecting explicit policy preferences across European tertiary systems. This methodological architecture was systematically situated within its historical lineage in 'Theoretical and empirical advances in the assessment of productive efficiency since the introduction of DEA: a bibliometric analysis' (2023), where Cavalcante Nepomuceno, Seixas Costa, and Daraio used co-citation and bibliographic coupling networks to trace five decades of theoretical evolution and identify application gaps across productive efficiency models.

## Latent Heterogeneity and Governance Dynamics in Higher Education

Daraio’s methodological work in frontier estimation finds primary empirical application in higher education governance. Traditional rankings routinely mischaracterize academic performance by confounding scale, institutional missions, and unobserved quality. In 'Quality as a latent heterogeneity factor in the efficiency of universities' (2021), Daraio, Simar, and Wilson demonstrated how directional distances can estimate an unobserved latent quality factor associated with human capital and managerial competence, showing that this latent factor shifts both the efficient frontier and the inefficiency distributions of European universities independently of institutional size. The operational realities of universities were further untangled by investigating the internal administrative apparatus. In 'The role of the non-academic staff heterogeneity in the performance of European universities' (2026), Avenali, Daraio, Di Leo, and Wolszczak-Derlacz showed that while the raw proportion of non-academic staff does not dictate technical efficiency, unobserved structural and qualitative heterogeneity significantly alters the boundary of the production set, revealing that unchecked administrative diversification triggers bureaucratic costs unless accompanied by effective professional integration.

This structural inquiry into university administration expands globally in 'Determinants of the incidence of non-academic staff in European and US HEIs' (2023), where Avenali, Daraio, and Wolszczak-Derlacz employed nonlinear regressions across balanced panels to demonstrate that institutional size, prestige, and revenue structures dictate non-academic staffing ratios across divergent European and American governance systems. At the micro-operational level, Daraio examined instructional performance in 'Teaching efficiency of Italian universities: A conditional frontier analysis' (2022), with Mastromarco and Toma, using robust conditional frontiers to assess course heterogeneity beyond crude ministerial metrics. Concurrently, Daraio examined the financial realities governing academic competition in 'Heterogeneity of national accounting systems, world-class universities and financial resources: What are the links?' (2024), where Avenali, Daraio, Di Leo, and Wolszczak-Derlacz showed how divergent national accounting standards distort Shanghai Ranking (ARWU) tiers, challenging resource-concentration mandates by demonstrating how financial leverage interacts with technical efficiency. This was extended in 'Climbing university rankings under resources constraints: a combined approach integrating DEA and directed Louvain community detection' (2025), where Di Leo, Avenali, Daraio, and Wolszczak-Derlacz integrated DEA with directed community detection algorithms to map feasible ranking trajectories for capital-constrained academic institutions.

This philosophical critique informs an empirical engagement with research assessment reform. Looking forward, Daraio’s program establishes a coherent horizon for the governance of knowledge-intensive societies. Whether modeling non-convex public production sets, engineering semantic knowledge bases to safeguard academic integrity, or reconstructing corrupted administrative registries, Daraio continues to expand operational research methodologies, providing structured analytical mechanisms for responsible, evidence-based public decision-making.
