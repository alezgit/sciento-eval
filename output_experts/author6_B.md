# Academic Narrative CV: Professor Chiara Grosso

## Architectures of Personalization and Circularity: The Evolution of Configuration Systems

The research trajectory of Professor Chiara Grosso investigates how digital configuration mechanisms bridge customer preferences, organizational capabilities, and socio-ecological imperatives. Her intellectual foundation is rooted in operations management and marketing informatics, specifically interrogating how web-based sales configurators can be structured not merely as transaction interfaces, but as value-generating cognitive environments. Over time, this focus on the micro-dynamics of product customization has systematically broadened into structural questions of organizational ambidexterity, socio-technical design ergonomics, and closed-loop material flows. Grosso’s scholarly agenda examines a continuous progression: from resolving information asymmetries between solitary buyers and complex manufacturing systems, through enriching customization environments with peer and expert social dynamics, to deploying algorithmic and multi-criteria configuration architectures to drive the European twin transition across sustainable industrial ecosystems.

At the core of Grosso's early scholarship is the deconstruction of value creation in mass customization. In 'Towards an understanding of how the capabilities deployed by a Web-based sales configurator can increase the benefits of possessing a mass-customized product' (2014), she and her co-authors established that the perceived value of a self-designed product extends beyond functional and aesthetic fit into non-utilitarian dimensions of uniqueness and self-expression. By analyzing user evaluations and empirical interactions with active web-based configurators, the study demonstrated that while focused navigation, flexible navigation, and easy-comparison capabilities elevate both utilitarian and hedonic-expressive value, capabilities such as user-friendly product space descriptions and benefit-cost communication primarily enhance utilitarian satisfaction. This structural division of configurator capabilities was subsequently corroborated in 'Enhancing the consumer-perceived benefits of a mass-customized product through its online sales configurator: An empirical examination' (2017), which evaluated consumer self-design experiences across multiple consumer product categories. The empirical findings critiqued prevailing commercial configurators as being excessively utilitarian-centered, neglecting design levers that cultivate symbolic personal value.

## The Mass Customization Imperative: Capability Bundling and Value Architecture

Grosso expanded the examination of user-facing configurator front-ends into the broader operations strategy of the firm, interrogating how organizations reconcile the conflicting demands of product flexibility, cost efficiency, delivery reliability, and product quality. Drawing on resource orchestration theory and the fit-as-covariation perspective, 'Reconciling Product Flexibility with Cost, Delivery, and Quality: The Importance of Bundling Mass Customization Practices' (2025) modeled the operational interdependence between three fundamental organizational practices: customer knowledge absorption, product modularity, and online sales configurator deployment. Employing a fit-as-covariation model to examine these organizational dynamics, the study established that bundling these three practices explains a substantial share of the variation in mass customization capability, markedly exceeding the explanatory power observed when these elements are analyzed as isolated, individual efforts. This finding validated the systemic premise that configurator tools cannot generate operational ambidexterity in isolation from upstream modular product architecture and organizational knowledge absorption.

This structural balance between front-end digital toolkits and back-end manufacturing reality also led Grosso to scrutinize context-specific sectoral adaptations. In 'Shoe configurators: A comparative analysis of capabilities and benefits' (2017), she conducted a comparative evaluation of footwear configuration toolkits against broader fashion sales interfaces, clarifying how real-time preview mechanisms calibrate customer expectations. Moving to heritage and localized production models, 'Configuration challenges for the “Made in Italy” Agrifood industry' (2021) examined the intersection between mass customization and traditional culinary consumption habits. By assessing configurator capabilities across active online platforms and evaluating digital practices across a broad sample of Italian agrifood small and medium enterprises, the research illuminated how regional provenance constraints and established consumption rituals create unique resistance to parametric customization, demonstrating that mass customization models require significant contextual adaptation when applied to culturally embedded consumer goods.

## Deconstructing the Isolated Customizer: Social Presence and Cognitive Mediation

Concurrently, Grosso extended this agenda through a theoretical critique of the traditional, isolated user paradigm. Recognizing that physical retail shopping inherently involves collaborative deliberation, her work introduced social computing paradigms to mass customization toolkits. In 'Support for the social dimension of shopping through web based sales configurators' (2015) and 'Supporting the social dimension of shopping for personalized products through online sales configurators' (2017), Grosso examined an extensive empirical sample of commercial online sales configurators, formulating a taxonomy of connection modalities between configuration engines and social software platforms. These studies revealed that the integration of social functionalities directly impacts specific configuration stages by providing social feedback and fulfilling users' social involvement needs, thereby replicating the consultative atmosphere of brick-and-mortar retail.

This line of inquiry developed into behavioral and ergonomic analyses of user psychology during the configuration journey. In 'Users' social-interaction needs while shopping via online sales configurators' (2019), Grosso analyzed configuration experiences across hundreds of commercial configurators, demonstrating that social needs are not uniform throughout the shopping journey. Instead, configurator users require distinct interaction modalities depending on whether they seek input from peers, professional experts, or the broader consumer crowd at discrete self-design stages. Grosso deepened this behavioral inquiry in 'Exploring Configurator Users’ Motivational Drivers for Digital Social Interaction' (2021), linking cognitive friction and decision-making complexity during product self-design to the psychological demand for social presence. Addressing the lag between evolving consumer expectations and enterprise platform agility, 'Enhancing users' digital social interactions while shopping via online sales configurators' (2023) proposed process-level personalization models, including the integration of trusted personal shopping advisors directly into the digital workspace to alleviate decision fatigue and reinforce purchase confidence.

## Algorithmic Guidance: Translating Configuration Toolkits to Human Development

Grosso subsequently translated the conceptual logic of modular product configuration into human-centered and socio-technical domains facing systemic structural disruption. In 'AI-implemented toolkit to assist users with career "configuration": The case of Create Your Own Future' (2022), she adapted configuration architectures and diagnostic artificial intelligence toolkits to the field of vocational counseling and workforce development. Prompted by labor market disruptions driven by Industry 4.0 automation and geopolitical displacements, the investigation analyzed how algorithmic recommendation engines can assess vocational personalities and dynamically configure personalized employment trajectories. By applying parametric option filtering, modular pathway design, and guided decision trees to human career adaptability, this study extended configuration principles beyond physical and digital commodities to social resilience, providing scalable diagnostic tools for vulnerable workers navigating dynamic employment landscapes.

## The Twin Transition: Digital Product Passports and Platform-Mediated Symbiosis

In her more recent scholarship, Grosso has consolidated her expertise in product configuration, multi-attribute decision theory, and platform design to address the challenges of the European twin transition—the convergence of digital transformation and circular economy principles. Central to this agenda is the operationalization of Digital Product Passports (DPPs) across complex industrial supply chains. In 'An Integrated Business Strategy for the Twin Transition: Leveraging Digital Product Passports and Circular Economy Models' (2025), she utilized the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) and the Technique for Order of Preference by Similarity to Ideal Solution (TOPSIS) to investigate how textile firms and strategic consultants prioritize circularity. The investigation identified three core operational priorities for DPP design: material traceability, repair guidance, and optimized end-of-life recycling pathways, while framing national designations like 'Made in Italy' as essential competitive differentiators.

This analytical focus expanded to consider consumer behavior and knowledge management within sustainability ecosystems. In 'Bridging digital tools and human skills: A sustainable competence framework for implementing digital product passports in the cosmetics industry' (2026), Grosso, alongside her collaborators, deployed hybrid multi-criteria methodologies (AHP, TOPSIS, and VIKOR) alongside consumer surveys to assess DPP implementation under the framework of United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 12. The analysis established that consumer digital literacy alters value perception: digitally literate consumers interpret passport data not merely as technical ingredient lists, but as symbolic markers of brand trust and corporate ethical responsibility. Complementing this, 'Integrating knowledge management and digital product passports to foster sustainable and collaborative ecosystems' (2026) applied k-means clustering, principal component analysis, and logistic regression across fashion and cosmetics value chains, identifying distinct consumer archetypes—aware versus unaware—and illustrating how DPPs act as collaborative knowledge infrastructures underpinning open innovation and supply chain resilience.

Economic incentives and regulatory governance frameworks form the operational boundary of this work. In 'Digital product passports for cleaner production: Economic evidence from producers and consumers in the fashion industry' (2026), Grosso evaluated multi-criteria industry priorities alongside consumer survey data, uncovering a clear willingness to pay a price premium for products embedded with verifiable digital labelling. Parallel to consumer-facing research, 'Advancing Business Strategy in End-Of-Life Management for the Fashion Industry' (2025) applied multi-criteria decision analysis across cross-sectoral stakeholders, demonstrating that policy considerations significantly outweigh technical and economic criteria when choosing textile end-of-life interventions, ranking reuse and mechanical recycling well above chemical recycling and landfilling. Translating these insights back into engineering design workflows, 'Designing for circularity: exploring configurator-based decision support for Eco-design in food packaging' (2025) introduced a rule-based configurator architecture capable of guiding eco-design decisions to comply with the European Union Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation. Finally, 'Enabling Closed-Loop Collaboration Through Configurators: A Digital Strategy for Circular Platforms' (2026) synthesized mass customization modularity with circular business models, mapping European circular platforms to demonstrate how digital configurators facilitate interorganizational waste exchange, component recovery, and industrial symbiosis.

Grosso’s investigation into circular decision support naturally extended toward systemic and extreme operational environments characterized by severe resource scarcity. In 'Current trends and challenges in construction and demolition waste recycling' (2025), she and her colleagues addressed urban material flows, reviewing how emerging computer vision and artificial intelligence triage systems optimize sorting efficiency and decision support in construction and demolition waste streams. ## Coordinated Frontiers: Ubiquitous Lifecycle Intelligence

The overarching trajectory of Professor Chiara Grosso’s scholarship traces a coherent evolution from consumer-facing mass customization toolkits to intelligent socio-technical architectures designed for resource conservation. Her work demonstrates that configurators, originally developed to reconcile operational efficiency with bespoke user desires, represent critical information bridges for resolving contemporary environmental challenges. By continuously linking computational decision methodologies (AHP, TOPSIS, VIKOR) with empirical behavioral science and organizational orchestration, Grosso has repositioned product configuration as an essential driver of the circular economy. Looking forward, her work delineates an integrated paradigm where product personalization, regulatory data governance, and lifecycle intelligence converge, providing an operational roadmap for sustainable production and consumption across complex global ecosystems.
