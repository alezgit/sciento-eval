## Evaluating Regulated Markets and Infrastructure Investments Under Volatility

The research program of Professor Daniele De Santis operates at the intersection of applied microeconometrics, regulatory economics, and transport infrastructure appraisal. Central to his scholarly inquiry is the rigorous modeling of organizational efficiency, cost dynamics, and capital allocation strategies within heavily regulated or state-subsidized network industries. By combining microeconometric frontier techniques, hybrid cost accounting frameworks, and stochastic real options models, De Santis investigates how public policy interventions and market uncertainties influence firm behavior, productivity, and technological transition.

His work systematically bridges theoretical economic modeling with empirical policy design, addressing critical trade-offs between fiscal efficiency, operational sustainability, and environmental goals. Across healthcare distribution and public transportation systems, his research establishes formal methodologies to assess regulatory mechanisms, optimize public funding structures, and guide long-term planning under technological and macroeconomic volatility.

## Microeconometric Frontiers and Regulatory Policy in Distribution Markets

A foundational pillar of De Santis's research examines the empirical consequences of statutory pricing interventions on firm-level productivity and financial viability in regulated distribution networks. In 'The pharmaceutical distributors’ efficiency in Italy: an assessment of the impact of the 2010 reimbursable drug pricing reform' (2025), he applies parametric Stochastic Frontier Analysis (SFA) to an extensive financial and operational dataset to evaluate the structural impact of statutory margin adjustments. His empirical findings demonstrate that statutory margin reductions failed to foster a more efficient market structure, revealing that larger distribution firms unexpectedly lagged behind smaller entities in long-run technical efficiency. Furthermore, the analysis illustrates that under current fixed percentage margin regulations, even highly efficient wholesalers are unable to earn normal economic profits, highlighting how rigid margin mechanisms that ignore true distribution cost structures can undermine long-term market viability and social welfare.

## Technological Heterogeneity and Hybrid Cost Accounting in Rail Systems

Building upon microeconometric efficiency estimation in regulated markets, De Santis extends his analytical scope to local public transport, developing hybrid costing paradigms that capture how engineering specifications dictate economic performance. In 'A hybrid cost model for light and heavy metro services' (2025), he constructs a hybrid cost accounting model utilizing comprehensive operational data to disentangle the unit cost structures of light and heavy rail networks. By explicitly integrating technological variables—such as the degree of vehicle automation, wheel technology, and peak hourly capacity—the model demonstrates that heavy metro systems, despite incurring higher absolute costs per vehicle-kilometer due to elevated capital depreciation, achieve superior cost efficiency on a per seat-kilometer basis at high demand thresholds. The study further demonstrates that infrastructure maintenance efficiency gains yield greater cost reductions than vehicle maintenance improvements, offering concrete benchmarks for setting competitive auction bases and economic compensation in transit procurement.

## Stochastic Capital Valuation and Fleet Decarbonization Under Uncertainty

The transition from deterministic cost modeling to dynamic capital allocation under deep uncertainty represents a significant trajectory in De Santis's work, particularly regarding public fleet electrification. In 'Investments in local public transport: A systematic literature review on evaluation methods with a focus on the role of uncertainty' (2025), he systematically maps the methodological evolution of transit appraisal, demonstrating that despite growing technological volatility and ecological transition imperatives, the literature overwhelmingly relies on static evaluation tools like Discounted Cash Flow and classic Cost-Benefit Analysis. To overcome these analytical limitations, De Santis formulates dynamic real options architectures that directly incorporate macroeconomic and technological stochasticity into fleet management decisions.

In 'Bus fleet decarbonization under macroeconomic and technological uncertainties: A real options approach to support decision-making' (2024), he introduces a multi-option Least Squares Monte Carlo (LSM) simulation model combined with Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) framing to evaluate path-dependent vehicle replacement strategies across alternative powertrain technologies. The analysis demonstrates that while conventional technologies remain cost-effective in the short term, operational flexibility and changing technology costs favor rapid electrification on inner-city and urban routes compared to long-distance commuter services. Extending this framework to public subsidy design, 'Public funding for bus system decarbonization accounting for technological uncertainty and depot infrastructure upgrades' (2026) models bus operators' adoption timing while explicitly accounting for initial depot infrastructure constraints and stochastic technological trends. The study reveals that while uniform high-percentage public subsidy schemes trigger immediate adoption, optimal fiscal efficiency is achieved through a differentiated funding structure—combining targeted vehicle purchase support with depot-tailored infrastructure grants—maximizing decarbonization speed while minimizing public fiscal expenditure.

## Integrative Horizons: Methodological Frameworks for Decision-Making Under Risk

The cumulative trajectory of De Santis's scholarship demonstrates a coherent progression from microeconometric efficiency assessment of regulated distribution networks to advanced stochastic decision support systems for urban infrastructure. By synthesizing parametric frontier analysis, engineering-based hybrid costing, and real option simulation techniques, his work consistently challenges static evaluation paradigms in public economics. His findings emphasize that sound regulatory intervention and infrastructure funding must explicitly account for technological heterogeneity, firm-level incentives, and operational uncertainty.

Looking forward, De Santis's research horizon points toward the development of adaptive policy mechanisms and resilient investment frameworks that integrate evolving decarbonization targets, digital transition demands, and systemic macroeconomic risks.
