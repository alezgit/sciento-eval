## Academic Biography

# Academic Narrative CV — Professor Simone Di Leo

Professor Simone Di Leo's research program sits at the intersection of **efficiency and productivity analysis**, **systematic review methodology**, and **sustainability-oriented applied economics**. Across these domains, his work is unified by a methodological commitment to **nonparametric and flexible benchmarking techniques** (notably Data Envelopment Analysis and its non-convex extensions), to **systematic and reproducible knowledge synthesis**, and to **empirically grounded policy assessment**. The narrative below reconstructs the intellectual trajectory, the methodological stance, and the inter-theme architecture of this body of work, drawing directly on the analysed publications.

---

## 1. Methodological Foundations: From Convex DEA to Conical Free Disposal Hull Estimators

The most theoretically committed strand of the program targets the **statistical foundations of efficiency measurement**. In *Conical Free Disposal Hull estimators of directional distances and Luenberger productivity indices for general technologies* (2025), Daraio, Di Leo and Simar move beyond the standard convexity assumption of classical DEA by proposing estimators of **directional distances to the cone spanned by the attainable set**, applicable to **general (non-convex) technologies** under Constant Returns to Scale. The contribution is not merely technical: by establishing the **statistical properties of these measures**, the authors enable the estimation of **Luenberger productivity indices and their decompositions** on non-convex attainable sets, with formal inference procedures validated on simulated and real datasets. This theme *underpins* every other empirical application in the program, because the productive-technology assumptions propagate into the validity of efficiency scores, conditional analyses, and policy benchmarks. The contrast with the conventional FDH literature is explicit: the conical FDH framework sacrifices convexity but retains a conical structure compatible with Luenberger-type indicators, thereby bridging the FDH and DEA traditions.

---

## 2. Benchmarking Knowledge-Based and Public-Sector Organizations

A second thematic pillar applies DEA, the **Balanced Scorecard (BSC)**, and **directed Louvain community detection** to heterogeneous decision-making units (DMUs) operating in the public and quasi-public sphere.

- **Universities as knowledge-based organizations** are studied along three axes. *Heterogeneity of national accounting systems, world-class universities and financial resources: What are the links?* (2024) shows that the financial resources under a university's control are strongly related to its position in the highest tier of the Shanghai Ranking, and proposes a predictive model — accounting for cross-country heterogeneity in national accounting systems — that maps financial resources onto ranking tier. *Climbing university rankings under resources constraints: a combined approach integrating DEA and directed Louvain community detection* (2025) operationalises this further by combining DEA (following the Banker-Charnes-Cooper tradition) with a directed Louvain community detection algorithm applied to 318 universities across five countries; the joint methodology identifies clusters of universities with similar financial profiles and corresponding ARWU scores, producing **realistic ranking targets** tailored to resource availability. The Italian case study (*A heuristic approach based on Leiden rankings to identify outliers: evidence from Italian universities in the European landscape*, 2023, with Daraio and Leydesdorff) complements the benchmarking work with a **heuristic, bibliometrically grounded procedure** for flagging outlier institutions, illustrated through a case study of Politecnico di Bari that attributes top-Leiden performance to small, young-led teams.
- The **epistemological underpinnings** of this benchmarking practice are addressed in *Meta-choices in ranking knowledge-based organizations* (2022), which dissects the three meta-choice problems — choice of algorithm, choice of variables, choice of data — and warns against treating multidimensional benchmarking as a purely technical exercise.
- The **operational building blocks** are consolidated in *DEA, balanced scorecard and intellectual capital including the gender dimension: A comprehensive list of indicators* (2023), a three-level methodology (systematic reviews + bibliometric mapping + grey-literature analysis) producing an inclusive indicator inventory classified along the dimensions of intellectual capital, with explicit treatment of the gender dimension.
- The **data infrastructure** required to do this work is documented in *An integrated dataset providing a landscape of the Italian Universities* (2023, with Bruni, Daraio, Ghamiloo and Bianchi), which assembles Scopus-derived research keywords (single words and bigrams), USTAT administrative variables, and unique balance-sheet data for the largest Italian universities over 2016–2018.
- The role of **non-academic, managerial and administrative staff** is investigated in *The role of the non-academic staff heterogeneity in the performance of European universities* (2026, with Avenali, Daraio and Wolszczak-Derlacz): a nonparametric approach extracts latent heterogeneity, which is then shown to shift both the efficient frontier and the inefficiency distribution, distinguishing universities that transform heterogeneity into a productive resource from those suffering bureaucratic overload.
- **Energy retail operators** are benchmarked in *A robust benchmarking of direct margin in Italy's energy retail markets* (2024, with Chicca, Daraio, Guerrini and Scarcella), where 120 Italian electricity and gas retailers are scored on cost and commercial efficiency using a direct-margin metric, with size emerging as a robust positive determinant of efficiency in the post-2016 retail-unbundling landscape.

This thread evolves from indicator construction (BSC + IC + gender, 2023) toward increasingly integrated methodological hybrids (DEA + Louvain, 2025) and toward a deeper, heterogeneity-aware treatment of DMU performance.

---

## 3. Waste Management, Eco-Efficiency and the Circular Economy

The third thematic cluster applies directional DEA and conditional efficiency analysis to **municipal and country-level waste systems**, with the circular economy as the unifying policy frame.

- *A Flexible and Sustainable Analysis of Waste Efficiency at the European Level* (2024, with D'Adamo, Daraio and Simar) uses **directional DEA** to benchmark EU countries on a (waste generated, recycled waste, landfilled/incinerated bad output) specification, with conditional targets reflecting social and economic heterogeneity; Central European countries dominate, and a collaboration-based optimisation is proposed.
- *Driving EU sustainability: Promoting the circular economy through municipal waste efficiency* (2024, with D'Adamo, Daraio, Gastaldi and Rossi) complements the macro analysis with an SLR (216 articles double-screened to 31) and a standard DEA on 27 EU countries over 2017–2021 using **economic, technical and sustainable DEA models**, identifying top-performer clusters (Netherlands, Slovenia, France, Italy, Germany, Sweden) and a stronger technical-sustainability synergy than economic-sustainability synergy.
- *Viable eco-efficiency targets for waste collection communities* (2024, with Daraio and Simar) introduces **conditional efficiency analysis** within Italian ATO waste-collection communities (89 municipalities of the Città metropolitana di Roma Capitale), producing **community-specific viable eco-efficiency targets** that respect local specificities while remaining on the eco-efficiency frontier.
- *Impact of a regulatory target and external factors on the waste efficiency of Italian municipalities* (2025, with Daraio and Simar) evaluates the 65 % sorted-collection target from Legislative Decree No. 152/2006 on 275 Italian municipalities over 2016–2019, showing that compliant municipalities exhibit higher cost efficiency as costs rise, with population and urban economic development as the strongest contextual drivers and a "neighbourhood solidarity" policy recommendation for non-compliant municipalities.

This thread mirrors the methodological progression seen in Theme 2: convex DEA is first deployed in macro applications, then replaced or supplemented by **directional** and **conditional** estimators when heterogeneity and non-convex technologies matter.

## 4. Photovoltaic, Battery Storage and Offshore Wind: Economic Sustainability of the Energy Transition

A fourth thematic cluster quantifies the **economic viability and policy sensitivity** of renewable-energy investments in PV, BES and offshore wind, using a coherent toolkit of **Net Present Value, sensitivity analysis, scenario analysis, break-even assessment, Monte Carlo simulation, and LASSO regression**.

- The **commercial/retail/restaurant** scale is analysed in *Green restaurants: An economic assessment of solar photovoltaics and energy storage systems* (2026, with D'Adamo, Gastaldi and Maccallini), where a 40 % capital deduction is shown to raise PV profitability from 425 to 1590 €/kW, and where BES becomes profitable only when self-consumption increases by 22–25 % with incentives and 30–35 % without them.
- The **commercial-building** scale is treated in *Advancing Sustainable Development Through Integrated Photovoltaic and Battery Energy Storage Systems in Commercial Buildings* (2025, with Antonini, D'Adamo, Gastaldi and Scarcelli), combining **multicriteria decision analysis** with the **discounted cash flow method** and a quantification of emission reductions; experts recommend a 50 % tax deduction, PV profitability ranges 548–1831 €/kW, and integrated PV+BES still delivers 77 tCO2eq avoided in year one.
- The **industrial** scale is covered by *Economic assessment of battery energy storage in industrial applications* (2026, with D'Adamo, Fabbi, Gastaldi and Kalogirou) — a 1500 kWh BES at a 1 MW PV pharmaceutical plant where profitability requires the simultaneous alignment of public support, CAPEX containment, and self-consumption uplift, with Monte Carlo confirming that the self-consumption lever dominates CAPEX reduction — and by *A policy-oriented framework for assessing the economic sustainability of industrial photovoltaic investments* (2026, with D'Adamo, Fabbi and Gastaldi), where a 45 % capital grant raises NPV from 1190 to 1610 €/kW and cuts the discounted payback from over seven to under two years, with monetised CO2 avoidance of approximately 37 000–63 000 €.
- The **residential/socio-behavioural** dimension is addressed in *Energy policy and citizens' perceptions* (2026, with D'Adamo, Fabbi, Gastaldi and Uricchio), an online survey showing that PV investment scores 8.75/10 as a sustainability priority versus 7.90/10 for PV+BES, that 55 % of respondents cannot modify consumption habits, and that women and respondents over 30 behave more sustainably.
- The **synthesis/SDG-7 framework** is provided by *Towards SDG 7: A Quantitative Sustainability Framework for Evaluating PV+BES Systems in the Low-Carbon Transition* (2026, with D'Adamo, Gastaldi and Scarcelli), applied to a Rome shopping mall, where NPV analysis, LASSO regression and greenhouse-gas-emission-reduction values jointly identify the avoided electricity-bill cost, the self-consumption rate and the replaced fossil-fuel mix as the dominant levers.
- **Offshore wind** is the focus of *Exploring the economic feasibility of offshore wind energy for sustainable development* (2025, with D'Adamo, Gastaldi, Ozturk and Terzi di Bergamo), with NPV 195–1324 €/kW, IRR 9–15 %, LCOE 125–138 €/MWh and the energy selling price, inflation rate and capacity factor as the key drivers.

Across these studies, the methodology is uniform but the scale of application moves from restaurants, to commercial buildings, to industry, to consumer perception, to SDG-level frameworks and offshore wind — a progression that mirrors the underlying technical-economic structure of the energy transition (decentralised prosumer adoption, industrial self-consumption, and large-scale renewable generation).

## 5. Sustainability Assessment of the Energy Retail Market and Composite Sustainability Indicators

Two further contributions anchor the program in the **sustainability-assessment** literature.

- *A Framework for the Analysis of the Sustainability of the Energy Retail Market* (2022, with Chicca, Daraio, Guerrini and Scarcella) bridges an SLR (48 papers, 1997–2022) with a bibliometric mapping to propose a conceptual framework that evaluates the technical, economic and environmental impacts of retail-energy policy decisions — a granularity absent in broader energy-market assessments.
- *Evaluating sustainability in Europe with composite indicators* (2025, with D'Adamo, Gastaldi and Paris) applies **min–max normalisation** and the **TOPSIS method** to 76 Eurostat SDG indicators for 2022, demonstrating strong consistency between the two methods and ranking Sweden, Denmark and the Netherlands at the top across the economic, environmental and social dimensions.

## 6. Circular Electronics, Digital Product Passports, and Digital Twins

Three recent contributions extend the program toward **circular-economy business models** and **digital transformation**.

- *From Awareness to Action: Consumer Behaviour, Attitudes and Business Strategy in Sustainable and Circular Electronic Devices* (2026, with D'Adamo, De Meester, Gastaldi and Lunadei) analyses 407 Italian consumer responses via an online questionnaire using statistical tests, PCA and **K-means clustering**, identifying generational and gender differences in repair, replacement and disposal behaviour, and recommending differentiated strategies (education for the young, economic incentives for the resource-constrained, premium programmes for eco-conscious consumers).
- *Integrating knowledge management and digital product passports to foster sustainable and collaborative ecosystems* (2026, with Becchi, D'Adamo, Gastaldi, Grosso and Trusiani) studies DPPs in Italian fashion and cosmetics via two online surveys combined with **K-means clustering**, **PCA** and **logistic regression**, reconceptualising the DPP as a socio-technical infrastructure integrating knowledge management, open innovation and circular-economy practice.
- *An Integrated and Flexible Review Framework to Evaluate the Evolution and Barriers of Digital-Twin Technologies in Industrial and Healthcare Domains* (2025, with Daraio and Orsini) applies the **Integrated Flexible Systematic Review (IFSR)** framework to 133 articles, mapping the **cross-domain spillovers** between industrial DTs (predictive maintenance, smart manufacturing) and healthcare DTs (personalised medicine, surgical simulation), and identifying interoperability, regulatory and computational barriers.

These three contributions share a **K-means/PCA/IFSR** methodological kit and a focus on **digital tools enabling circular, sustainable ecosystems**.

## 7. Methodology of Systematic Reviews: Meta-Knowledge and AI ChatBots

The final thematic cluster concerns the **epistemology and tooling of systematic reviews** themselves — a meta-level theme that conditions every other empirical and SLR-based contribution in the program.

- *Systematic reviews as a metaknowledge tool: caveats and a review of available options* (2023, with Avenali, Daraio, Matteucci and Nepomuceno) reviews available SLR tooling and the state-of-the-art coverage problem, arguing that rigorous SLR requires the intelligent combination of automated tools with domain-expert knowledge, illustrated through a review of *International Transactions in Operational Research*.
- *Pitfalls, benefits, and comparative analysis of artificial intelligence ChatBots in the systematic review process* (2026, with Daraio and Ferazzoli) applies a **case-study methodology** to the same *International Transactions in Operational Research* corpus, benchmarking **NLP-enabled AI ChatBots** against traditional manual SLR stages and reporting empirical evidence on integration pitfalls and benefits across the SLR workflow.

This theme *underpins* the SLR and bibliometric evidence base used throughout the program, from university-bibliometric datasets to digital-twin and waste-management reviews.

## 8. Data Quality, Integration and Error Detection in Longitudinal Datasets

A cross-cutting technical strand develops **data-quality** and **longitudinal-data-cleaning** machinery that is operationalised in the program.

- *Accounting for quality in data integration systems: a completeness-aware integration approach* (2022, with Daraio and Scannapieco) proposes a **completeness-aware** integration layer for multinational, historical data systems that maximises the information accessible to the final user at the integration layer, illustrated on a research infrastructure for science and innovation studies.
- *Flexible Techniques to Detect Typical Hidden Errors in Large Longitudinal Datasets* (2024, with Bruni and Daraio) introduces a **statistical-mathematical system of indicators** that captures elusive errors (surviving trends, fluctuations, symmetries/asymmetries of consecutive differences) and is applied to the ETER database ("Total academic staff", "Total number of enrolled students") for European Higher Education institutions.

These methods are the **infrastructural substrate** on which the Italian-university and European-university analyses rest.

## How the Threads Interlock

- The **conical-FDH estimators** of Theme 1 supply the statistical foundation for the **directional and conditional DEA** deployed in Themes 2 and 3; the contrast with convex DEA is not a methodological curiosity but a substantive choice when the technology is plausibly non-convex (university production, municipal waste systems).
- The **data-quality and error-detection** thread (Theme 8) makes the cross-country, longitudinal benchmarking of universities (Theme 2) and waste systems (Theme 3) credible; without completeness-aware integration and elusive-error detection, the comparator samples would be biased.
- The **systematic-review methodology** thread (Theme 7) underwrites the bibliometric evidence base in Themes 2, 3 and 6 (Leiden/Scopus keyword extraction, the DPP literature, the digital-twin literature, the waste-management SLR), while the **energy-transition economic-viability** thread (Theme 4) is internally self-consistent, using the same NPV / Monte Carlo / LASSO kit across multiple scales.
- The **sustainability-indicator** thread (Theme 5) provides the cross-cutting normative lens that connects energy-retail sustainability, EU-level composite sustainability, and the SDG-7 framework of the energy-transition cluster.
- Over time, the program has moved from **convex DEA applications** (university ranking, retail margin) to **directional/conditional/conical FDH** applications, from **single-DMU efficiency** to **community- and system-level benchmarking**, from **SLR as a one-off tool** to **SLR as a meta-knowledge discipline** benchmarked against AI chatbots, and from **sector-internal efficiency** to **sustainability assessment integrated across economic, environmental and social dimensions**.
